# @xmoq/media

What stands between a LOC video track and the codecs of a browser: from a subscription to a decoder, and from the chunks of an encoder to a subscription. "LOC" is `draft-ietf-moq-loc-04`, "MSF" `draft-ietf-moq-msf-01`, and "transport" `draft-ietf-moq-transport-18`.

Status: partly implemented. A page plays the H.264 video of `moqlivemock`'s publisher with this in Chromium, in the interop run ([record](../../docs/experiments/2026-10-11-a-page-plays-the-video-of-mlmpub.md)): a subscription, the receiver, the decoder of WebCodecs. A page encodes frames with the H.264 encoder of WebCodecs and publishes them, and another page plays them, through the own relay and the moqtail relay, in the interop run ([record](../../docs/experiments/2026-10-11-pages-send-and-play-video.md)). Drawing a frame and taking one from a camera are the caller's: the demo's pages draw the frames, and the publishing page takes them from a camera with `?source=camera` (`demo/broadcast/publish.ts`).

## Scope

| Export | Where | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `createVideoReceiver` | `src/core/video-receiver.ts` | The Objects of a video track go in, in any order, and the frames come out in the order a decoder takes them. `abandonGroup` is for the caller that has the decoder, when it failed or fell behind |
| `createVideoSender` | `src/core/video-sender.ts` | The chunks of an encoder go in, and what comes out says for each which Object it is, with which properties, and whether a stream is opened or finished for it |
| `createVideoTrackPublisher` | `src/moqt/video-track-publisher.ts` | Answers the SUBSCRIBE of a relay for a video track, and writes the chunks the caller hands it through the sender. The encoder is the caller's |
| `createVideoTrackEncoder` | `src/web/encode-video-track.ts` | That publisher and a `VideoEncoder` that feeds it. The caller hands in frames (`VideoFrame`), from whatever source |
| `playVideoTrack` | `src/web/play-video-track.ts` | Subscribes to a video track, puts its Objects through the receiver and gives the frames to a `VideoDecoder` (below) |
| `createAudioTrackPublisher`, `createAudioTrackEncoder`, `playAudioTrack` | `src/moqt/audio-track-publisher.ts`, `src/web/audio-track.ts` | Audio, in its smallest form (below) |
| `createPlayout`, with `createPlayoutClock`, `createFrameQueue` and `placePiece` | `src/web/playout.ts`, and `src/core` for what decides when each is played | Picture and sound played together (below) |
| `keepPublishing`, `keepPlaying`, `keepTrackOnSession` | `src/moqt/keep-broadcast.ts` | A publisher and a viewer over more than one session (below) |
| `stayOnSession` | `src/moqt/stay-on-session.ts` | A viewer that waits on its session (below) |
| `createQualitySwitch` | `src/core/quality-switch.ts` | Which of several versions of one video a viewer plays (below) |
| `createStamp`, `createFastestWay`, `deliveryTimeoutParameters` | `src/core/stamp.ts`, `fastest-way.ts`, `delivery-timeout.ts` | A source's times on the wall clock, which Objects are of the player's clock, and the parameters of the delivery timeouts |

**`playVideoTrack`**: the caller gets each decoded `VideoFrame` and closes it. With it comes where it came from (`FrameOrigin`: its Location and the Timestamp of LOC it carried), looked up among the last chunks given to the decoder (`MAX_FRAMES_REMEMBERED`, **provisional**), and undefined beyond them: the frame is handed on all the same. The codec string is the caller's to hand in, from the catalog. It rejects before it subscribes where the browser has no decoder for that codec (`VideoDecoder.isConfigSupported`). A decoder that fails is closed and made anew, and the playing ends at the `DEFAULT_MAX_DECODER_FAILURES`-th failure in a row; one that does not keep up (`DEFAULT_MAX_DECODE_QUEUE`) loses the rest of a Group. `joiningFetch` and `newGroupRequest` are in Known gaps. **When the receiver says that the track has ended (`end-of-track`), that is counted and the subscription goes on**: it ends when the publisher ends it, or the caller.

**Audio, in its smallest form**: every chunk of an `AudioEncoder` is a Group and a stream of its own, an Object with the Timestamp of LOC (`DEFAULT_AUDIO_CHUNKS_PER_GROUP`, decided on 2026-10-13, with what it rests on; `chunksPerGroup` asks for another number). Its subscriptions are taken as the video's are (the rows "Subscriptions", "The Largest Object of SUBSCRIBE_OK" and "A FETCH for the track" below; a FETCH after `end` is refused with DOES_NOT_EXIST), and `end` waits for the encoder and the writes `endTimeoutMs` at most each (`DEFAULT_END_TIMEOUT_MS` of the video's publisher). **Nothing of the relay or of a viewer gives a stream of sound up unless asked to**: the relay gives nothing up unless a flag or a viewer asks it to, and the demo's page and `xmoq-watch` ask for a delivery timeout for the video alone. **The weakness to know**: where the relay gave older Groups up for every track alike with no grace, on a link narrower than the video, a chunk a stream had 204 to 375 of 1,000 pieces sound, where ten a stream had 855 to 898 ([record](../../docs/experiments/2026-10-12-a-page-behind-a-link-with-too-little-room-and-a-relay-that-gives-groups-up.md)); with the timeout asked for the video alone, as the viewers do, 969 to 983 sounded with a chunk a stream ([record](../../docs/experiments/2026-10-12-the-relay-follows-the-delivery-timeout-a-viewer-asks-for.md)); a chunk that cannot be written in time is left out. The player decodes the Objects in the order of their times and drops one whose time is not after the last, and one whose time is over 2^53 - 1 microseconds (`beyond-time`, `MAX_DECODER_TIME` in `src/core/decoder-time.ts`, the video's limit as well); a chunk the decoder throws for is dropped as `decoder-refused`, and playing goes on; with `holdForOrderMs`, an Object after a gap waits that long for the ones before it (`src/core/in-order.ts`; nothing waits when it is left out). Opus at 48,000 Hz, mono, was run, page to page through the own relay ([record](../../docs/experiments/2026-10-11-opus-audio-page-to-page.md)). **No `description` is sent or given to the decoder** (Chromium's encoder gives one of 19 bytes; whether it is to travel is undecided). `createAudioTrackPublisher` has unit tests (`test/audio-track-publisher.test.ts`); the encoder and `playAudioTrack` are run only in a page. No Audio Config or Audio Level of LOC. Audio and video are kept in step by `createPlayout`.

**Picture and sound played together**: `createPlayout` takes the decoded frames of `playVideoTrack` and the decoded pieces of `playAudioTrack`, holds each for one buffer after the time it carries, draws the frame at a repaint with the caller's `draw`, and gives the piece to an `AudioContext`. When each is played is decided without I/O, in `src/core`: `createPlayoutClock` (the one clock and the buffer), `createFrameQueue` (the frames that wait) and `placePiece` (where a piece of sound begins). The section "How picture and sound are played" has the rules.

**A viewer that waits on its session (a trial)**: with `stay` on `keepPlaying` (`stayOnSession`), a `play` that fails as "not yet" (the caller says which failures are) and a playing that has ended are asked for again on the same session, and the session is not closed. The pauses are those of `createAskAgain` of [`@xmoq/moqt`](../moqt/README.md): after an end, or a refusal that names no time, `DEFAULT_ASK_AGAIN_DELAY_MS`; after a refusal with a Retry Interval, that long within the bounds there (**provisional**). For a viewer whose SUBSCRIBE a relay holds until a publisher is there (RENDEZVOUS_TIMEOUT, which `followCatalog` of `@xmoq/msf` sends with `rendezvousTimeoutMs`); the demo's `/watch?wait=1` is that viewer ([record](../../docs/experiments/2026-10-13-a-page-that-waits-for-the-broadcast-at-the-relay.md)). Left out, nothing changes.

**A publisher and a viewer that go on over more than one session**: `keepPublishing` and `keepPlaying`, on the loop `keepMoving` of `@xmoq/moqt`, until the caller's `signal` is aborted. When the next session is tried (`againMs`, `DEFAULT_RECONNECT_DELAY_MS`, **provisional**) and what a GOAWAY of the relay (transport §10.4) does are the rules of `createNextSession` and `keepMoving`, in the [README of `@xmoq/moqt`](../moqt/README.md). `keepPublishing` answers the requests for its tracks and announces the namespace anew on every session; the encoders are the caller's and go on meanwhile, and what they make while no session is there is not sent later. `keepPlaying` calls the caller's `connect` and `play` anew, also where only what is played ended (its publisher went); with one `createPlayout` for all sessions, the picture goes on by the one clock. A GOAWAY on the stream of the video's subscription ends `playVideoTrack` with `moved`, which `movedBy` of `keepPlaying` takes as one on the control stream; one on the sound's has `playAudioTrack` cancel it, and `keepTrackOnSession` asks for it again on the session (the row "GOAWAY" of `docs/moqt-implementation-notes.md`). Run in the interop run with the own relay stopped and started anew ([record](../../docs/experiments/2026-10-12-pages-connect-again-when-the-relay-comes-back.md)). **Not there**: the viewer does not ask for what it missed, a publisher does not say which Groups it left out, and nothing limits how long it is tried. **A track that ends alone**: `keepPlaying` follows the `ended` its `play` gives, which in the demo is the video's; `keepTrackOnSession` plays another track, the sound, on the same session and subscribes to it again when its subscription ends or is refused, by the rules of `stay`, so that a relay that ends the sound alone does not leave the picture without it, and the picture does not stop for it.

**Several versions of one video (a trial)**: `createQualitySwitch` says which of the versions a viewer plays, from how late the picture comes: one lower at once when it is later than `DEFAULT_DOWN_LATE_MS` or no frame comes, one higher when it was less late than `DEFAULT_UP_LATE_MS` for `DEFAULT_UP_AFTER_MS`, nothing for `DEFAULT_HOLD_MS` after a change, and a try upwards that fails within `DEFAULT_UP_AFTER_MS` doubles the wait before the next, up to `MAX_UP_AFTER_FACTOR` times the first. **Every value is provisional.** With `tryMs` (off when left out) a higher version is tried first: the caller receives it next to the one it plays (`trying()`), tells `tick` how late its picture comes, and plays it only when the try held for that long; a try that fails is not seen and comes again after the same wait ([record](../../docs/experiments/2026-10-12-a-try-before-going-up-that-comes-again-as-soon.md)). It plays nothing itself: the demo's watching page subscribes to the other track and leaves the one before at the new one's first frame. For a publisher that encodes the same frames for several tracks, `createVideoTrackEncoder` has `wantsKeyFrame()` and `encode(frame, { keyFrame: true })`, so that a key frame one track is asked for is made on all and their Groups begin together ([record](../../docs/experiments/2026-10-12-two-versions-of-the-video-and-a-viewer-that-changes-between-them.md)).

**Changing the timeout while a track plays**: the playback of `playVideoTrack` has `setDeliveryTimeouts`, which sends a REQUEST_UPDATE for the subscription (transport §10.9) and resolves when the sender accepted. The XmoQ relay accepts an update with nothing but delivery timeouts and NEW_GROUP_REQUEST, acts on `subgroupMs`, and the new time holds for the Groups that end after it (the rows on REQUEST_UPDATE of `docs/moqt-implementation-notes.md`); **a sender that refuses an update ends the subscription** (§10.9.1). `playAudioTrack` has no such call.

**Delivery timeouts**: `playVideoTrack` and `playAudioTrack` take `deliveryTimeouts` (`subgroupMs`, `objectMs`) and send them with the SUBSCRIBE as SUBGROUP_DELIVERY_TIMEOUT and OBJECT_DELIVERY_TIMEOUT (transport §8); nothing is sent without it. The own relay acts on `subgroupMs` (a Group of video on one stream is given up that long after its end) and not on `objectMs`; the moqtail relay acts on neither ([what the draft has](../../docs/experiments/2026-10-12-delivery-timeouts-of-a-subscription-what-draft-18-has-and-who-does-what.md), [the relay](../../docs/experiments/2026-10-12-the-relay-follows-the-delivery-timeout-a-viewer-asks-for.md)).

Not implemented: a source of frames (a camera, a screen), a canvas of its own (the caller draws).

## Layer rules

`@xmoq/media` is layer 2 of the packages (`package_layer` in `scripts/ci/check-layers.sh`): it may use `@xmoq/loc`, `@xmoq/moqt` and `@xmoq/msf`, and of the packages only `@xmoq/meeting`, layer 3, may use it. `src/core` is numbers and bytes: it imports `@xmoq/loc` alone, no connection and no WebCodecs. `tsconfig.core.json` type-checks it without the DOM library, and `noRestrictedImports` in the root `biome.jsonc` keeps `@xmoq/moqt`, `@xmoq/msf` and `src/web` out of it. An Object goes in with the shape of `ReceivedObject` of `@xmoq/moqt`, which a test hands in as that type. `src/moqt` uses a connection of `@xmoq/moqt` and no WebCodecs. `src/web` is the part that needs a browser: a connection of `@xmoq/moqt` and WebCodecs.

## How the receiver decides

Where a draft says it, the sentence is quoted. **Much of this no draft has as a rule**: it is what this receiver does, provisional, and to be changed with measurements. Why each is so is at `createVideoReceiver` and the options it names.

| Point | What is done | From |
|---|---|---|
| The order of the frames | The frames of a Group come out in the order of their Object IDs, from Object 0, without a gap; Groups in ascending order, and one that was passed does not come back. An Object that is ahead of its turn is held | MSF §6.2: "Object ID MUST be zero for the first Object within a Group and then MUST increase monotonically by one within that Group." **Both ways of putting Objects onto streams are read**: a Group on one stream, which the sender here and `moqlivemock` do, and every Object on a stream of its own, which MSF §6 asks for and where a later Object can come first (the open proposal `moq-wg/msf` #211 has "subscribers MUST be prepared to consume any of them"; at `DEFAULT_MAPPING`) |
| Which frame a decoder can begin with | Object 0 of a Group, marked `key`. Nothing in the payload is looked at | **Not written as a rule anywhere, and taken as a promise of the sender**: transport §2.3, MSF §4.1 and the example of LOC-04 §4.2, where "shall" is no key word, point to it (`VideoReceiverEvent.key`). The own sender and `moqlivemock` keep it. The Video Frame Marking of LOC, which could say it of a frame, is not read |
| A receiver that comes in the middle of a Group | Waits for Object 0 of a later Group. What it gets before is held, and dropped when that comes (`no-key-frame`) or when the limits are reached (`overflow`) | Nothing before a key frame can be decoded. The receiver asks for nothing; `playVideoTrack` can fetch the start of the Group or ask for a new one (Known gaps) |
| A frame that does not come | Where the publisher did not say that it does not exist (the rows on End of Group and End of Track), it counts as not coming **when Object 0 of a later Group is there**, or when more is held behind it than the limits. Its Group ends there: what was held after it is dropped (`gap`), and the next frame is Object 0 of a later Group | No clock is at hand here. The transport has ways to say that an Object does not exist (an Object Status, the END_OF_GROUP bit of a subgroup stream, the Prior Group ID Gap and Prior Object ID Gap properties of §12.8 and §12.9); **`moqlivemock` was not seen to send one, and this receiver reads the Object Status alone**. **The cost: a frame at the end of a Group that comes after the next Group's Object 0 is dropped, though it was not lost**; with every Object on a stream of its own this can happen without loss. Having the later Group wait for it, as long as the player's buffer leaves, was tried and not put in: it showed no more frames, and fewer on a link with much loss ([record](../../docs/experiments/2026-10-12-a-later-group-of-video-waits-for-the-one-before-it.md)) |
| A frame that cannot be used | Ends its Group as well: it is dropped as `unusable`, with what was wrong, and what was held after it as `after-unusable`. Nothing is closed for it | Unusable is: properties `readLocTiming` of `@xmoq/loc` finds `invalid`, a payload `toAnnexB` finds `invalid`, a payload of no bytes, **no Timestamp**, and a time over 2^53 - 1 microseconds |
| An Object without a Timestamp | Unusable | LOC-04 §2.2 has the properties as "optional metadata", so this is narrower than LOC. `moqlivemock` sends one on every Object. The Timestamp is looked for inside Immutable Properties as well (transport §12.7), by `src/moqt/loc-properties.ts` before the receiver is given the Object |
| The time of a frame | Microseconds, at most 2^53 - 1 (`MAX_TIMESTAMP`). With a Timescale of the Object, by that; else with the Timescale of the track, which the caller hands in (`trackTimescale`, from 1 to 2^64 - 1); else the Timestamp as it is, wall-clock time | LOC-04 §2.3.1.1, and §6.1, where TIMESCALE has the scope "Track, Object": that an Object's own goes before the track's is read into that, and the draft does not say which wins. **A track can have a Timescale in two places**, the Track Property of LOC and `timescale` of the catalog (MSF §5.2.21), and no draft says which holds where they differ (`trackTimescale`) |
| The payload | Through `toAnnexB`: a length before every NAL unit becomes a start code, and a payload with start codes stays as it is | The decoder is configured for Annex B, without a description ([record](../../docs/experiments/2026-10-11-loc-video-payloads-in-chromium.md)). **What this counts on, and does not check**: the track is H.264, which the caller knows from `codec` of the catalog; the parameter sets are in the payload of the key frames, not in a Video Config or in `initData` of the catalog, neither of which is read; and a start code has 4 bytes. What `toAnnexB` does not look at is in the README of `@xmoq/loc` |
| End of Group, as an Object | Taken when it arrives, whatever came before it, and no frame itself. Nothing at or after it in the Group is waited for: what is held there is dropped (`after-end`), and what comes there later as well (`after-end`, or `late` once the Group is over). Of two for one Group, the lower holds. **The subscription is not cancelled for such an Object** | Transport §11.2.1.1, and §2.1: "All signals that an Object does not exist are authoritative". §2.4.2 has an Object after the end of its Group make a Track malformed, for which "a subscriber ... MUST cancel any corresponding subscription", and §2.1 has "an endpoint can receive an Object after it has already recorded that the Object does not exist ... This is not a protocol error". This receiver cannot tell the two cases apart and only drops the Object; cancelling is for what holds the subscription |
| End of Track, as an Object | **Told as an event when it arrives**, whatever came before it; frames before its Location can still follow. Nothing at or after its Location is a frame: what is held there is dropped (`after-end`), and what comes there later, in its Group and in every later one (`after-end`, or `late` once its Group is over). The subscription is not cancelled, as above | Transport §11.2.1.1, and §2.1 and §2.4.2 as above. **Nobody else checks this**: `@xmoq/moqt` finds an Object after an End of Track on the same subgroup stream, and not one that comes on another stream |
| A second copy of an Object | Dropped as `late`, the first one kept | The rule of no draft |
| How much is held | `DEFAULT_MAX_HELD_OBJECTS` Objects and `DEFAULT_MAX_HELD_BYTES` of payload (`maxHeldObjects`, up to `MOST_HELD_OBJECTS`, and `maxHeldBytes`). Over that, the Group that is played is given up where it waits for a frame; without one, the oldest Group that is held goes, and can still begin when its Object 0 comes. As many ends of Groups are remembered as Objects may be held, those of the highest Groups | **Provisional, not measured.** **An Object over `maxHeldBytes` by itself is never held**; `@xmoq/moqt` takes larger Objects unless the viewer sets `limits.maxObjectPayloadLength` lower, which it is to do (Known gaps) |

## How the sender decides

Why each is so is at `createVideoSender` and the options it names.

| Point | What is done | From |
|---|---|---|
| Groups | A key chunk begins a Group, as its Object 0; a delta chunk is the next Object of that Group. Groups are numbered one after the other from `firstGroup`, which the caller gives. A key chunk when the last Group ID there is was used is not sent | MSF §4.1, and §6.1 (at `firstGroup`). That a Group begins with its key frame is the promise the receiver above counts on |
| Streams | **A Group on one stream, Subgroup 0, finished when the next Group begins** (`mapping: "stream-per-group"`, `DEFAULT_MAPPING`; decided on 2026-10-11). `mapping: "stream-per-object"` puts every Object on a stream of its own, in a Subgroup whose ID is its Object ID (transport §2.2, §11.4.2) | **The default leaves a MUST of MSF-01, on purpose.** §6: "Irrespective of the Section 4 in place, each MOQT Object MUST be mapped to a new MOQT Stream". Why, which senders were read, and where the sentence stood in `moq-wg/msf` (issue #148, pull request #211) are at `DEFAULT_MAPPING`. The other mapping stays as a setting, for a peer that sends or expects the letter of §6 |
| The properties of an Object | The Timestamp of LOC, and nothing else: `epoch` plus the encoder's time, in wall-clock microseconds, without a Timescale. At most 2^53 - 1, which is what the receiver above hands on; `epoch` is from 0 to that | LOC-04 §2.3.1.1. **The encoder's time is not wall-clock time**, so a caller cannot hand it on as it is (`epoch`). **The catalog of such a track has no `timescale`** (MSF §5.2.21): a receiver that is given one reads these Timestamps in that unit, and the one here then finds every frame beyond the time it hands on |
| The payload | The data of the chunk, unchanged. **It has to begin with a start code of 4 bytes and have something after it**; a chunk that does not is not sent. Provisional | The codec is H.264 in Annex B form ([roadmap](../../docs/roadmap.md)). LOC allows a start code of 3 bytes (§2.1.4) and a length before every NAL unit (§2.1.3) as well; why this sender writes one form is in `createVideoSender`. Chromium 156 begins its chunks with 4 bytes ([record](../../docs/experiments/2026-10-11-loc-video-payloads-in-chromium.md)); what stands before the later NAL units of a chunk was not looked at |
| A chunk that is not sent | Nothing of its Group is sent after it: the next Object is the next key chunk. Not sent are a delta chunk before the first key chunk, a chunk over `maxPayloadLength`, one without the start code, one whose time is no whole number of microseconds or lies outside 0 to 2^53 - 1 with `epoch`, and a key chunk for which no Group ID is left (`VideoSkipReason`). The Group that is cut ends where it was cut; no Group ID is left out, and a stream that is open for it is finished, as every Object the Group has was written | **`maxPayloadLength` is `DEFAULT_MAX_PAYLOAD_LENGTH` without a setting, provisional**: what passes the relay here ([implementation notes](../../docs/moqt-implementation-notes.md), "Undecoded bytes of subgroup streams"). How large a key frame of a camera picture is was not measured |
| An Object that could not be written | The caller says so (`endGroup("write-failed")`): nothing more of the Group is sent, and a stream that is open for the Group is **reset**, not finished. When the track ends (`endGroup("track-ended")`) it is finished. With a stream for every Object, the stream of the Object that failed is the caller's to reset | Transport §11.4.3 |

## How a track is published

`createVideoTrackPublisher`, in `src/moqt`: the chunks of an encoder go through the sender, and its steps are written to the subscription a relay made. No WebCodecs; the encoder is the caller's, and so is the catalog that names the track (which is to have no `timescale`, the row on the properties above).

| Point | What is done | From |
|---|---|---|
| Subscriptions | **One at a time.** A second SUBSCRIBE while one is open or being answered is refused with DUPLICATE_SUBSCRIPTION; after the first ended, one is accepted again. FORWARD 0: accepted, nothing is written, and the end of the track is told. **The Forward State is as the SUBSCRIBE had it**: `@xmoq/moqt` refuses a REQUEST_UPDATE that changes it. Of a REQUEST_UPDATE, the NEW_GROUP_REQUEST is read (row below) | A relay makes one subscription for all its viewers. One sender numbers the Groups of the track, and a write that fails for one subscriber would cut the Group for another. Transport §5.1 for FORWARD 0 |
| The Largest Object of SUBSCRIBE_OK | The last Object the chunks made, also when nobody was subscribed then; none before the first | Transport §10.2.11 |
| A subscriber that comes in the middle of a Group | Gets nothing of that Group: its first Object is the next key chunk, which `push` asks the encoder for, once, with the first chunk after the subscriber came | `moqlivemock`'s publisher does the same |
| A FETCH for the track | Refused with NOT_SUPPORTED | No frame is kept here. A viewer of this package sends a Joining FETCH; where a relay has no cache, it comes to the publisher and is refused, and the viewer has its picture from the next key frame on |
| A subscriber that asks for a new Group (NEW_GROUP_REQUEST, transport §10.2.13) | Only with `dynamicGroups`, which also puts DYNAMIC_GROUPS = 1 into the SUBSCRIBE_OK (§12.6). A request of 0, or of a value larger than the Group being sent, has `push` ask for a key chunk once the Group is `shortestGroupMs` long (`DEFAULT_SHORTEST_GROUP_MS` of the chunks' time when left out, decided on 2026-10-21); a key chunk that comes first meets it. With a shortest Group of 0, the first decoded frame came 63 to 97 ms after the SUBSCRIBE in the demo (10 viewers; the rest of the measurements are in Known gaps, [record](../../docs/experiments/2026-10-14-a-viewer-asks-for-a-new-group-instead-of-fetching.md)). Without `dynamicGroups` a request does nothing | **Off when left out**: the demo does not set it |
| The steps of the sender | In the order of the chunks, each behind the one before it. An Object that opens a stream: `openSubgroup`, with the Subgroup ID said by the mode of the header, 0 for the stream of a Group and the first Object ID for the stream of an Object; never marked as the end of its Group, which is not known before the next key chunk | `SubgroupWriter`: "Wait for a write before the next." Transport §11.4.2 for the modes |
| A write, or an `openSubgroup`, that fails | Where the subscription is over, or the session: nothing, the subscriber is gone. For anything else, a subscriber that stopped the stream among it: the stream is reset, what was queued of the Group is not written, nothing is sent before the next key chunk, and `push` asks for one. A failure in a Group that is over cuts nothing of the Group after it. Each such failure is counted (`writeFailures`), an `openSubgroup` that rejects with `StreamNotOpenedError` where the peer allows no further stream among them: that stream is not waited for or asked for again | Transport §11.4.3 for the reset |
| A subscriber that does not take what is sent | When `DEFAULT_MAX_PENDING_OBJECTS` Objects or `DEFAULT_MAX_PENDING_BYTES` wait to be written (`maxPendingObjects`, `maxPendingBytes`), the chunk is not sent and its Group ends there, as after a failure. **What waits of that Group is not written**, and a write of it that is under way is given up by resetting its stream; what waits of the Group before it is still written. `maxPendingBytes` is not below the longest payload that is sent | **Provisional, not measured** |
| Asking for a key frame | `push` says so once when a Group was cut short, and not again before a key chunk came. It does not say so while more than half of what may wait still waits | `askForKeyFrame` of `VideoPushResult` |
| The end of the track (`end`) | What is queued is written, the stream of the Group finished, and PUBLISH_DONE (TRACK_ENDED) sent. What is not written within `endTimeoutMs` (`DEFAULT_END_TIMEOUT_MS`) is not written: its stream is reset, and what is under way then has the same time once more to end. **`end` resolves after `done` of `@xmoq/moqt` has, which waits for the subscriber to end its direction of the request stream** as well. A SUBSCRIBE that is being answered when the track ends gets PUBLISH_DONE once it is answered, and `end` waits for that | Transport §10.11, and §5.1.1: PUBLISH_DONE waits for the streams to be closed or reset. **Where a stream could not be opened or reset in that time, PUBLISH_DONE is not sent**, and the failure is reported with `reportError` |

## How frames are encoded

`createVideoTrackEncoder`, in `src/web`. No test without a browser, for the reason `playVideoTrack` has none; the interop run is where it is checked.

| Point | What is done | From |
|---|---|---|
| The encoder | `VideoEncoder` with `{ codec, width, height, avc: { format: "annexb" }, latencyMode: "realtime" }`, and `bitrate` and `framerate` where the caller gives them. Refused before anything is made where the browser has none for it (`VideoEncoder.isConfigSupported`) | Annex B is what the sender sends. `avc1.42E01F` at 320x180 was run, in Chromium 156 on macOS |
| The codec string | The encoder is configured with the caller's (`avc1.…` was run). **The catalog of the track is to say `avc3.…`** with the same profile and level (decided on 2026-10-11; why at `codec`) | The catalog is the caller's; this does not write one |
| Key frames | The first frame; a frame `keyFrameIntervalMs` or more after the last key frame, by the frames' own times; and the next frame after the publisher asked for one. **`keyFrameIntervalMs` has no value when left out** | `keyFrameIntervalMs` |
| A subscriber that comes | The publisher asks for a key frame with the first chunk after it came, once, unless that chunk is one. The subscriber's first Object is that key frame | **This is for the publisher's subscriber, which is the relay**: a relay subscribes once for all its viewers, so a viewer that comes later still waits for the next key frame |
| An encoder that is behind | A frame that comes while more than `DEFAULT_MAX_ENCODE_QUEUE` wait in the encoder (`encodeQueueSize`, `maxEncodeQueue`) is closed and not encoded. **Provisional, not measured** | No Group is cut for it. In the interop run 9 of the first frames went this way once, in the first run of all; in no run after it, at 1280x720 and up to 70,000,000 bits a second among them |
| The time of an Object | The wall-clock time at which the first frame came, plus the time of the frame since the first one, as the frames say it. The first frame's own time need not be 0 | The Timestamp of LOC is wall-clock time (the table of the sender) |
| A source with a clock of its own | `clock: "least"` of both encoders (`StampClock`, `createStamp`; **not the default, and not measured with real devices**): the wall clock is read with every frame, and the source's times are moved by the smallest of "wall clock less the source's time" among the frames of the last minute, the rule of `createFastestWay`. A time is never at or before the one given last, where the wall clock is set back by less than 10 seconds. The sound's times are counted from the samples handed to the encoder, as the encoder numbers its chunks | Why, and why not the other ways, at `StampClock` in `src/core/stamp.ts` |
| Key frames that cannot be sent | When `DEFAULT_MAX_UNSENT_KEY_CHUNKS` key chunks in a row (`maxUnsentKeyChunks`, **provisional**) were not sent for what they are (`too-large`, mostly), `onKeyChunksNotSent` is called, once until a key chunk was sent again. The encoder and the sending go on. A key frame is asked for once after the first that was not sent, and not again before one was sent (the row "Asking for a key frame") | Nothing of a track reaches anybody without its key frames, and the counts alone would say so. WHY it goes on: the key frame of another picture can be smaller. WHY not asked for again and again: it would be as large, and every frame would become a key frame |
| A frame whose time is not after the one before it | Not encoded (`time-not-after`) | The receiver and a decoder take the times to go up, and the key frame interval is counted in them |
| The end (`end`) | The encoder is flushed, for `endTimeoutMs` at most, and closed; then the publisher ends the track | An encoder that stands still is not to keep `end` from resolving |
| An encoder that fails | Not made anew: `failed` resolves with the error, and no frame is encoded from then on. **Provisional**; no encoder was seen to fail | `failed` |

## How picture and sound are played

`createPlayout`, with `createPlayoutClock`, `createFrameQueue` and `placePiece`. **Every value here is the one the demo had, provisional, and from one machine** ([delay](../../docs/experiments/2026-10-11-where-the-delay-of-the-demo-comes-from.md), [sync](../../docs/experiments/2026-10-11-demo-sync-and-delay.md)). Why each value is what it is, is at its constant.

| Question | What it does | Why |
|---|---|---|
| One clock for both | The time of a frame or a piece is the publisher's, in microseconds. Its base on the viewer's clock is that time plus the smallest difference between the two clocks **among what came in the last minute** (`FASTEST_WINDOW_MS`, by whole seconds of the viewer's clock; provisional; `fixtures/playout/least.tsv`); a frame is shown `bufferMs` after its base, a piece sounds `audioBufferMs` after | With one base and one buffer, picture and sound with the same time come out together. Kept for good, the smallest lost a viewer with the faster clock all of its sound, after 7 to 10 minutes at 100 ppm and 50 ms by what 1,000 and 10,000 ppm did. With a minute the viewer is behind by what the clocks move in it, 6 ms at 100 ppm; 1,000 ppm (60 ms) kept the sound with 150 ms and the automatic buffer and not with 50 ms, and 10,000 ppm lost it as before. **When a fastest way that was too fast is forgotten, everything is due later at once, by as much as it was off**: with 340 ms the picture stood for a third of a second (20 frames shown in that second) and the sound had one gap, 2 breaks counted, no piece too late. A link that is bad for longer than the minute may have its delay taken for the way; a narrow link of 90 s did not do that visibly ([record](../../docs/experiments/2026-10-13-a-publisher-and-a-viewer-whose-clocks-run-at-different-speeds.md)) |
| A time that is not of the clock | A frame or a piece whose time is more than `MOST_FROM_THE_FASTEST_MS` from where the fastest one puts it, sooner or later, is left out and moves nothing (`createFastestWay`; `baseOf` gives undefined, `onStray` tells); the `ANEW_AFTER`-th of them in a row begins the clock anew, and so does one that had nothing of the clock for a minute. `playAudioTrack` does the same before it puts the pieces in order (`not-of-the-clock`) | **Provisional**, the values of the viewer in a terminal (`crates/watch/src/clock.rs`) |
| The buffer | `DEFAULT_PLAYOUT_BUFFER_MS` when left out (**decided on 2026-10-21**); set while playing with `clock.setBuffer`. The frames that wait follow it at once | The cost: the first picture on a good link after about 208 ms, not 105 with 50 ms ([record](../../docs/experiments/2026-10-14-a-buffer-of-100-ms-beside-50-150-and-the-automatic-one.md)) |
| The sound with a small buffer | Waits `DEFAULT_AUDIO_MIN_MS` at least (`audioMinMs`), also where the buffer is less: it is then behind the picture by the difference | `DEFAULT_AUDIO_MIN_MS` |
| The automatic buffer (`auto`, `clock.setAuto`; **a trial, not the default**) | What `DEFAULT_AUTO_SHARE` (`autoShare`) of the frames and pieces of the last `AUTO_WINDOW_MS` came within, after the fastest, and `AUTO_MARGIN_MS` more, in steps of `AUTO_STEP_MS`; `DEFAULT_AUTO_MAX_MS` at most (`autoMaxMs`); not moved by fewer than `AUTO_MIN_SAMPLES`. It grows at once and shrinks a step at a `tick`, twice a second. `createPlayout` ticks with a timer of the page, which a browser slows in a background tab: the buffer shrinks more slowly there. **Two trials behind options, off when left out**: `autoDown: "quarter"` (down a quarter of what is left at a tick) and `autoWindowMs` (how far back it looks); both come down sooner after a bad spell and lose more sound when the next one comes ([record](../../docs/experiments/2026-10-13-the-automatic-buffer-what-raises-it-and-how-fast-it-comes-down.md)) | With loss a few of 100 come seconds late, and a buffer that waits for those is not live |
| Frames that wait | `DEFAULT_MAX_WAITING_FRAMES` at most (`maxWaitingFrames`); the oldest is closed for one more. At a repaint the newest frame whose time has come is drawn, and the ones before it are passed over, also one whose own time has not come: the picture never goes back (`due` of `frame-queue.ts`) | A tab in the background is not repainted, and its frames would wait without end |
| Where a piece of sound begins | At the end of the piece before it, to the sample, while that one still sounds and ends within `ABSORB_SECONDS` of the piece's own time. More than `SEAM_SECONDS` apart, the piece is played up to `STRETCH` slower or faster until the sound is at its own time again. Else at its own time; a piece whose beginning is over is not sounded | `placePiece` |
| A piece that waits for the ones before it | `clock.holdForOrderMs` for `playAudioTrack`: the buffer of the sound less `ORDER_MARGIN_MS` | What the decoder and the loudspeaker need is left |
| Sound before a click | `startSound` makes the `AudioContext`, and is to be called from a click. Until then the pieces are counted as what would have sounded (`onPiece`) | A browser lets a page make sound only after one |

The automatic buffer and where a piece of sound begins are what the viewer for the terminal (`crates/watch`) does as well: both read the rows of [`fixtures/playout/`](../../fixtures/playout/README.md) in their tests, and a rule is changed for both.

Limits on what the publisher decides: `MAX_LATENESS_SAMPLES` arrivals are kept for the automatic buffer, and a chunk of sound is dropped while more than `MAX_AUDIO_DECODE_QUEUE` wait in the decoder (`decoder-behind`); both **provisional, not measured**. `playVideoTrack` by itself hands every frame on with the time it carries: the limit on times is the playout's.

Not here: video does not wait for a Group that is late (tried, [record](../../docs/experiments/2026-10-12-a-later-group-of-video-waits-for-the-one-before-it.md)); nothing conceals a piece of sound that is missing; **`createPlayout` has no test without a browser**, and is run by the demo (`bun demo/check.ts`); nobody judged the sync by eye and ear.

## Known gaps

- **A publisher whose video and sound are timed by two clocks loses one of them at the viewer.** The fastest way is one for both tracks, so the track whose clock runs faster keeps renewing it and the other is later and later: sound that is too late and stays so, or frames drawn as they come and, once they are `MOST_FROM_THE_FASTEST_MS` off, none at all (the sound goes on, so there are never `ANEW_AFTER` in a row to begin anew by). Seen with the demo's `/publish?clockSkewPpm`, which moves the painted clock's times and not the tone's ([record](../../docs/experiments/2026-10-13-a-publisher-and-a-viewer-whose-clocks-run-at-different-speeds.md)); how far a real camera and microphone drift was not measured. Forgetting the fastest way after a minute does not mend it: the two tracks' times do move apart. **The sender can hold it, behind a choice that is not the default**: with `clock: "least"` both encoders follow the wall clock, and a source 1,000 ppm fast or slow stayed where it began or 60 ms above it, where it ran away by 300 ms in 5 minutes without ([record](../../docs/experiments/2026-10-13-an-hour-of-the-demo.md)). A slow source is behind by what its clock loses in a minute. Not run with a camera or a microphone. Not run either, and read from the rule: with `"least"`, a wall clock that is set far ahead, or a source that leaves samples out (its count of them then falls behind the wall clock), has times that are behind by that much for up to a minute and then move by it at once; where that is more than `SEAM_SECONDS` a viewer of this package reads a break in the sound there.
- **A publisher cannot send to several viewers without a relay**: it has one subscription.
- **A track whose key frames are all over `maxPayloadLength` sends nothing.** Seen in the interop run with 1600x900 of noise: 13 key chunks of about 1,005,000 bytes were not sent (`too-large`), the 271 chunks after them neither (`no-key-frame`), and the viewer had no frame. `createVideoTrackEncoder` tells its caller (`onKeyChunksNotSent`) and goes on; **what to do then is the caller's, and nothing makes the frames smaller**. `createVideoTrackPublisher` alone tells nobody: its `push` says `too-large` for each. A key frame of 988,000 bytes went through both relays.
- **`moqlivemock`'s subscriber cannot write the video of this sender out.** The sender writes payloads with start codes; `mlmsub` v0.16.1 takes a LOC video payload to have a length of 4 bytes before every NAL unit, as `moqlivemock`'s publisher sends it, fails on the first Object and reads the track no further (with `-videoout`; without, it takes the Objects). LOC-04 has both forms. The receiver here reads both.
- **A viewer that comes while a track runs has its picture at once only where something answers its Joining FETCH, or the sender begins a Group for it.** **Decided on 2026-10-21: the Joining FETCH when nothing is said, and the request only when asked.** With `newGroupRequest` (off when left out) `playVideoTrack` sends NEW_GROUP_REQUEST 0 with its SUBSCRIBE, and a sender with `dynamicGroups` begins a Group. `playVideoTrack` sends a Joining FETCH (`joiningFetch`, on when left out) for the Group it comes into; a relay that keeps the latest Group answers it, and the frames of the Group so far go to the decoder at once, without the limit on what may wait in it. Where nobody answers (no relay, a relay without a cache, a publisher of this package, which refuses a FETCH), the viewer waits for the next key frame, up to the key frame interval (a second with `moqlivemock`). Seen in the demo: the first frame 3 to 4 ms after the SUBSCRIBE was accepted with the FETCH, 114 to 804 ms without (four viewers each). From the SUBSCRIBE to the first decoded frame, 10 viewers each: with Groups of 1 s, 1 to 3 ms with the FETCH, 15 to 480 ms (median 96) with the request, 47 to 1,028 ms (median 552) with neither; with Groups of 4 s, 1 to 4 ms, 63 to 579 ms (median 83) and 373 to 3,973 ms. The fetched frames are from the start of the Group, up to a Group older than live; with the request the first frame is live, and each request that is met costs a key frame ([record](../../docs/experiments/2026-10-14-a-viewer-asks-for-a-new-group-instead-of-fetching.md)). Over 50 ms each way the FETCH gave the first picture within 83 ms and the viewer caught up within a quarter of a second; waiting took up to a whole Group, and the sound was heard that long before the picture ([record](../../docs/experiments/2026-10-12-the-first-picture-of-a-viewer-who-comes-late-fetch-or-wait.md)). **Not looked at**: a Group longer than the receiver holds (`DEFAULT_MAX_HELD_OBJECTS`, `DEFAULT_MAX_HELD_BYTES`) while the FETCH comes, and what the burst of old frames looks like to a person.
- **The unit tests of `createVideoTrackPublisher` use a subscription made by hand** (`test/fake-track.ts`, which says what it refuses and where it is more lenient than the real one). With a browser and a relay it runs in the interop run, where no write fails and no subscriber is behind: what it does then has the unit tests alone.
- **A subscriber that comes while the SUBSCRIBE of it is answered misses a key chunk that comes meanwhile.** The publisher asks for a key frame with the first chunk after a subscriber has come, so the wait is the encoder's delay and not the key frame interval; where what is asked for is not acted on (a caller that ignores `askForKeyFrame`), the subscriber waits for the next key frame the encoder makes by itself.
- **The Forward State cannot change while a track is published.** `PublishedTrack` of `@xmoq/moqt` has the parameters of the SUBSCRIBE, and `@xmoq/moqt` refuses a REQUEST_UPDATE with anything but delivery timeouts and NEW_GROUP_REQUEST, which ends the subscription.
- **A viewer that comes in the middle of a Group was not run with a decoder.** `moqlivemock`'s publisher begins a subscription at the start of a Group, so the page of the interop run never had an Object to drop before its first key frame; the tables of the receiver have that case, and no run.
- **The decoder was not made to fail or to fall behind in a run that is kept.** That a decoder that fails is closed and made anew, and that the playing ends at the `DEFAULT_MAX_DECODER_FAILURES`-th failure in a row, was seen with temporary changes that gave the decoder frames it cannot take (a decoder whose `decode()` threw was still `configured`, and is closed here); a decoder that recovers, and one that is too slow, were not run.
- **`playVideoTrack` has no test without a browser.** A decoder made up for a test would take what the real one refuses; the interop run is where it is checked.
- **The connection of a viewer is to be made with `limits.maxObjectPayloadLength` of 1 MiB or less** (the row on how much is held). `playVideoTrack` is handed a connection and cannot set that. The demo's pages and the interop run with the noise set it; the interop run with the bars does not.
- **After an End of Track, what is held before it stays held**: Objects that wait for a frame before them are not dropped when the track has ended, as no later Group can begin.
- **A Group that was cut short is not marked as ended.** The sender gives no End of Group, and nothing tells a receiver that the Group is over but the next Group's Object 0: with a Group on one stream as well, where the FIN says that the Subgroup is over and not that the Group is.
- **After a restart the sender does not say which Groups it left out.** MSF §6.1: "If a publisher maintains state across a restart and knows the previous Group ID, it SHOULD signal the gap using the MOQT Prior Group ID Gap Extension header." The Objects have the Timestamp alone.
- **No chunk of a real encoder is among what the tests of the sender expect.** The frames of `moqlivemock` go through it in the tests; the chunks of Chromium go through it in the interop run, and none is kept in `fixtures/`.
- **An Object after an End of Group or an End of Track does not cancel anything** (the table of the receiver).
- **The sender does not keep to a frame rate or a size of a Group**: a source that sends no key chunk makes one Group without end.
- **Of what a publisher can say about Objects that do not exist, only the Object Status is read.** The END_OF_GROUP bit of a subgroup stream does not reach the receiver (`ReceivedObject` has no place for it), and the Prior Group ID Gap and Prior Object ID Gap properties are passed over: a Group with a gap that its publisher announced is given up like one with a loss.
- **The end of the subscription is not told to the receiver** (PUBLISH_DONE with TRACK_ENDED, which MSF §11.3 ends a broadcast with). That is for what connects it.
- **One Object 0 of a Group far ahead makes every Group below it late.** Groups only go up, so a publisher, or a fault, that sends one Group ID much too high stops the picture until the Group IDs have caught up.
- **Held Objects are held without a time limit.** A track that stops in the middle of a Group keeps up to `maxHeldObjects` Objects until the receiver is let go.
- What the size of an Object may be is not this package's: the connection has that limit (`limits.maxObjectPayloadLength` of `@xmoq/moqt`).
- Only H.264 was run. LOC-04 §2.1 has the two forms of the payload for H.265 as well; nothing here was tried with it.
