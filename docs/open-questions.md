# Open decisions

Do not infer answers.

- When to move the MoQ implementation from draft 18 to a newer draft: **undecided**
- When a video frame counts as lost at the receiver: **provisional, not measured** (`@xmoq/media`). A frame that has not come when Object 0 of a later Group has is not waited for, and one that comes after that is dropped though it was not lost; besides that, at most 32 Objects and 4 MiB are held behind a missing frame. No clock is used. To be decided with a buffer for playback, in Phase 2.
- The default playout buffer of a viewer (the page's and the terminal viewer's): **50 ms, provisional; asked on 2026-10-13, not decided**. Behind a link narrower than the video every piece of the sound comes, 65 to 105 ms after the fastest, so with 50 ms about three of four are left out as too late; with 150 ms none are. The automatic buffer settles at 40 ms on a good link and goes to its limit of 400 ms behind a narrow one, and nobody has listened to the sound it plays at a changed speed ([record](experiments/2026-10-13-the-sound-behind-the-video-on-a-narrow-link-and-priorities.md)). The choices put: 50 ms as it is, the automatic buffer, 150 ms, or listening first.
- Whether a video Object without a LOC Timestamp is played: **not played, provisional**. LOC has the property as optional; the receiver has no other time for a frame. An Object's own Timescale is taken before the track's, where the drafts do not say which wins.
- What video chunks the sender takes: **provisional** (`@xmoq/media`). One that does not begin with a start code of 4 bytes is not sent: LOC §2.1.4 allows 3 bytes, which the own receiver would read as a length. One of more than 1,000,000 bytes is not sent (this bound is decided, 2026-10-11: it stays, and the caller is told when key chunks cannot be sent, `onKeyChunksNotSent`; the rest of this item is provisional): the relay takes a little under 1 MiB of a subgroup stream that is not decoded yet, which counts the fields before a payload and what a read brought of the next Object; the number was read from the relay's code and not run against it. Where the sender is set to a stream for every Object, the Subgroup ID is the Object ID. A Group that the sender cuts short gets no End of Group: a receiver learns that it is over from Object 0 of the next.
- How a video track is published and played in a browser: **provisional values, not measured** (`@xmoq/media`). The publisher holds at most 32 Objects and 4 MiB that are not written yet, cuts the Group when that is reached and asks the encoder for a key frame, and takes one subscription at a time. It waits 3 seconds (`endTimeoutMs`) when it ends for what is queued, and the same once more for what is under way. The player makes a new decoder when one fails and gives up after three failures in a row without a frame; it skips to the next key frame when more than 8 chunks wait in the decoder. The encoder's side drops a frame before encoding when more than 2 wait in the encoder (`maxEncodeQueue`); frames were lost to it while the encoder started, in one run of seven, and in no run with large pictures. A key frame interval has to be given; the interop runs give 1 second.
- What is left open of the relay's cache. Decided on 2026-10-11: the latest Group of a track, 8 MiB and 4,096 Objects for one track and 256 MiB for the relay, and 30 seconds after the last publisher has gone. **The 30 seconds are not built, by a decision of the same day**: a viewer that comes in that time could not read anything with them. The relay cannot accept its SUBSCRIBE without a publisher (draft 18 §9.4: "The relay MUST have an Established upstream subscription before sending SUBSCRIBE_OK in response to a downstream SUBSCRIBE"), a Joining FETCH needs a subscription that is Established or Pending and is refused with it (§10.12.2), and `followCatalog` sends its FETCH only after its SUBSCRIBE was accepted. What is left is a Standalone FETCH by a viewer that already knows the Group's number, which no client here sends. The value stays for when something uses it; what helps a publisher that breaks and comes back is letting a SUBSCRIBE wait for it (RENDEZVOUS_TIMEOUT, §10.2.6, a known gap). How it is built is in the row on the relay's cache in the [implementation notes](moqt-implementation-notes.md): a Group over the bound is given up and asked of the publisher, the Group before the latest is dropped when a later one begins, and the Objects of every publisher of the track go in. **Provisional**: a track with MAX_CACHE_DURATION among its Track Properties, or with Immutable Properties, which the relay does not read into, is not cached at all, and stays so once any of its publishers has sent either. Not measured: what the 256 MiB do not count (the fixed size of an Object apart from its payload and properties, and what a FETCH being answered still holds)
- What is left of the codec: **undecided** (the video codec of Phase 1 is H.264 in Annex B format, the packaging follows LOC and MSF, and the audio codec is Opus, see the [roadmap](roadmap.md)). Facts for the decision are in the [WebCodecs loopback record](experiments/2026-10-10-webcodecs-loopback.md) and, for the other browsers, in the [record of browser support](experiments/2026-10-10-browser-support-webtransport-webcodecs.md). The parts to decide:
  - The H.264 profile and level, and whether the hardware encoder is asked for, left to the browser, or avoided
  - Key frame interval, and whether a receiver can ask for a key frame
  - What a receiver does when a chunk is missing (H.264 and VP8 decoders show corrupt pictures without an error)
  - `latencyMode` and `optimizeForLatency`; whether `duration` travels; whether resolution may change during a broadcast
  - Whether the Opus `description` is passed to the decoder (a fact: Chromium decodes without it, [record](experiments/2026-10-11-opus-audio-page-to-page.md))
  - Decided on 2026-10-11: ten chunks of audio share a Group and a stream. **What it was decided on did not hold** (the sound was not late with a chunk a stream; the decoder's times were early), and the number was not decided again: what is known now is at `DEFAULT_AUDIO_CHUNKS_PER_GROUP` in `@xmoq/media`. Measured again on 2026-10-12 with 1, 5 and 10 ([record](experiments/2026-10-12-one-five-or-ten-pieces-of-sound-a-group.md)): nothing measured speaks for 10; 1 a Group is the same without loss and at 1% loss, loses fewer pieces on a link narrower than what is sent and at 5% loss with a short buffer, and gives the relay about a half more to do
- Receive limits of the MoQ implementation (Object size, buffered bytes per stream, concurrent streams, key-value pairs per message, drain timeout after PUBLISH_DONE, the time a stream of the peer gets to say what it is, the time the answer to a request gets, the time a request waits for its stream and how often a browser is asked again, requests of the peer open at once and how many of them are FETCHes, writes of one stream under way) and the limits of the relay's routing (announced namespaces per session, subscribers waiting for one upstream answer, requests open on one publisher, the time a publisher gets to answer, the time a client gets for its SETUP, the time a session may hold nothing, how long and how many SUBSCRIBEs are held for a publisher to appear, the time a peer gets to end its direction of a request stream the relay has finished) and of the Rust client (the time it waits for its session) : **provisional values in the code**, marked as such and listed in [implementation notes](moqt-implementation-notes.md). What happens when one is exceeded is decided and is in the same document
- Idle timeout of the relay: **6 seconds, provisional**, with a keep-alive every 3, so that a peer that vanished is noticed within 9 seconds. QUIC takes the smaller idle timeout of the two ends, so the clients time out after 6 seconds without a packet as well. Whether that is too short for a viewer on a poor link is to be measured on real links. Not in Phase 1: its demo runs on loopback (decided on 2026-10-10), and where a relay would be hosted is undecided.
- Which congestion controller the relay uses. Decided on 2026-10-12: **Cubic stays the default, and `--congestion` chooses `newreno` or `bbr`**. WHY NOT BBR as the default: it is better only where packets are lost at random, it is worse where the link's rate is the limit, which of the two a viewer's path is like is not known, and `quinn-proto` 0.11.19 calls its BBR experimental. To be looked at again with runs over real links. Neither is better on every link measured, all on one machine: where packets are lost at random BBR keeps up and Cubic falls behind for good ([record](experiments/2026-10-12-video-over-a-link-that-delays-and-loses.md)); where the link's rate is the limit and its queue drops, Cubic is as good or better and BBR sends more than the link takes ([record](experiments/2026-10-12-cubic-and-bbr-over-a-link-with-too-little-room.md)). Not known: which of the two a viewer's path is like. `quinn-proto` 0.11.19 calls its BBR experimental. Whichever is taken, nothing thins the frames when the link has less room than the video
- What the relay counts a slow subscriber by: **per subscriber session today (4 MiB, 4096 Objects and 256 streams waiting to be written, over all the tracks the session subscribes to), provisional; to be decided in Phase 1**. The subscription whose stream reaches the limit is the one that ends, so one track can use up the session's allowance and get another track of the same session cut off: a video track with many short Subgroups could end the audio subscription of the same viewer. The choices are a count per subscription, per session as now, or both.
- How many sources one track may have at the relay: **4, provisional**. A PUBLISH beyond that is refused with `EXCESSIVE_LOAD`. Every source costs the relay a subscription and its streams, whether anything of it is passed on or not; what number a deployment needs depends on how many publishers send one track, which is not known.
- How often a session may announce a namespace at the relay: **five `PUBLISH_NAMESPACE` requests at once and one more each second, provisional** (`--announce-at-once`, `--announce-every-ms`); one beyond is refused with `EXCESSIVE_LOAD`. The numbers are not measured against a use: every own client and the peers of the interop runs announce once or twice for a session. Each session has its own allowance, and no limit holds for the sessions together ([implementation notes](moqt-implementation-notes.md)).
- How much of a publisher one subscriber's session may take at the relay: **8 of the 64 requests the relay has open on a publisher, and 4 of its 16 FETCH requests, provisional** (`--requests-for-one-session`, `--fetches-for-one-session`). A viewer of the demo has 3 tracks and one or two FETCHes at a time. The numbers are not measured against a deployment, and a sender with several sessions is not counted as one ([implementation notes](moqt-implementation-notes.md)).
- What a refusal says of when to ask again (Retry Interval): **after a second with `EXCESSIVE_LOAD`, and at the relay with `DOES_NOT_EXIST`; at once with the relay's `TIMEOUT`; provisional**, and not randomized. A viewer that waits for a broadcast at a relay that does not hold its request asks once a second by it ([implementation notes](moqt-implementation-notes.md), `fixtures/retry-interval/`).
- How many publishers a track remembers as asked for it without becoming a source, so as not to ask them with every subscriber: **16, provisional**; and when to ask one again: **when it announces a namespace of the track anew, today**. The relay keeps no time after which it tries again and does not look at a Retry Interval, so a publisher that was only slow is left out until it announces again ([implementation notes](moqt-implementation-notes.md)).
- How long the relay remembers which stream a Subgroup was taken from: **the 8 newest Groups and 256 Subgroups of a track, provisional** ([implementation notes](moqt-implementation-notes.md)). The numbers decide how late a second copy of a Subgroup can come and still be recognised.
- Whether a Subgroup whose stream was cut short is continued from another source of the track: **not done; to be looked at in Phase 2 with measurements**. The draft allows it (§2.2: a second stream for a Subgroup is permitted when "one of the streams was reset prematurely"; §11.4.3: a relay "might attempt to recover the missing Objects"). Today the Subgroup ends for the subscribers where its stream was cut, and the next Subgroup is taken from whichever source sends it first.
- How a viewer who joins mid-broadcast gets its first picture: **undecided**, to be compared in Phase 1 with video. The draft (§5.1.3) offers waiting for the next Group, asking the publisher for a new Group (NEW_GROUP_REQUEST, which needs REQUEST_UPDATE), and fetching the current Group from a relay cache (needs FETCH and a cache). The relay keeps the latest Group of a track and answers a FETCH from it where it holds the Group without a hole; the comparison is to be made with it and with video Measured on one machine and over 50 ms each way ([record](experiments/2026-10-12-the-first-picture-of-a-viewer-who-comes-late-fetch-or-wait.md)): with the FETCH the first picture comes within 83 ms and is up to a Group old, the viewer catches up within a quarter of a second, and the start of the Group is decoded for nothing; waiting takes up to a whole Group (1 s and 2 s with those key frame intervals), and the sound is heard that long before the picture.
- Whether a slow subscriber should lose whole old Groups and stay subscribed (delivery timeout, §8 of the draft) instead of having its subscription ended: **to look at in Phase 2**, with measurements of how often subscriptions are ended. Tried on 2026-10-12 behind a flag of the relay that is off by default (`--delivery-patience-ms`): with Cubic the video stops falling behind and loses the end of each Group; with BBR the viewer gets less, at 2 Mbit/s nothing ([record](experiments/2026-10-12-the-relay-gives-up-a-group-a-viewer-does-not-get-in-time.md)) **Provisional since 2026-10-12**: the relay acts on the SUBGROUP_DELIVERY_TIMEOUT a subscriber sets on a SUBSCRIBE (§8), counted from when it knows a Subgroup is whole, with `--delivery-patience-ms` as what a subscriber gets that sets none and the smaller of the two where both are there; `--older-groups give-up` stays a flag for every subscriber. A viewer that asks for 100 ms on its video alone, at a relay with no flag, has what "give up at the next Group with 100 ms of grace" gave, and keeps its sound whole ([record](experiments/2026-10-12-the-relay-follows-the-delivery-timeout-a-viewer-asks-for.md)). Still open: what a viewer asks for, whether the two flags are kept, and whether any of it is a default. **The demo's watching page asks for 150 ms on its video since 2026-10-12, provisional** ([why](experiments/2026-10-12-which-delivery-timeout-a-viewer-asks-for-on-its-video.md)); `playVideoTrack` asks for nothing unless told
- Whether a browser client should end a subscription itself when its application does not read: **undecided**. The client holds back instead: it reads a bounded number of streams and leaves the rest to the transport, so that the relay ends the subscription (`TOO_FAR_BEHIND`). Facts are in the [backlog record](experiments/2026-10-10-subscription-backlog-in-chromium.md): the relay did end it in every shape of traffic tried, but with many streams of one large Object each the page was not told until it read again, and the answers to other requests of the same session, a SUBSCRIBE_OK among them, did not arrive either. Runs with Objects of the size of a video frame (10,000 bytes, 30 a second) showed that this is not a matter of the shape of the traffic: a page that never reads was cut off after 61 seconds with one Object a stream and after 171 seconds with a Group of 30 on a stream, the browser held about 16 MB and about 47 MB by then (from the relay's counts), and in both the page was not told, got no answer to its next request, and lost the session two minutes later when the browser refused a stream. A page that reads at half the rate is cut off as well (after 87 and 198 seconds) and is told. So how Phase 1 cuts Objects into streams moves when this happens, not whether, and the decision here has to be made in Phase 1
- How much of a fetch stream is held for a reader that does not take it: **provisional**, 80 KiB a stream (five reads of 16 KiB) in the Rust reader, next to the stream's receive window. For a FETCH the relay passes on, all **provisional**: 16 at once for a session, and 16 at once towards one publisher; 5 seconds for the publisher's answer, and FILL_TIMEOUT again from the answer (5 seconds without one, 1 second at least, 30 at most) for its fetch stream to open; FILL_TIMEOUT, 5 seconds without one and 30 seconds at most, for a publisher that sends nothing on its fetch stream; 10 seconds for a subscriber that takes less than 1 KiB; 96 KiB of the stream held in the relay; 64 REQUEST_UPDATEs that wait for the answer, in the relay and in `@xmoq/moqt`; 10 seconds for the SUBSCRIBE of a Joining FETCH that was read first to come, which is the time a stream of the peer gets to say what it is. A FETCH has no time limit as a whole. The two 16 are the same number, so one session can hold every FETCH a publisher is asked for, for as long as it keeps taking 1 KiB every 10 seconds; a smaller number for a session (4, say) and a time limit for a FETCH as a whole are to be decided with the other limits, before Phase 1
- How long a peer has to end its direction of a request stream after this endpoint finished its own (a refusal, a served FETCH, PUBLISH_DONE): 3 seconds in `@xmoq/moqt`, 2 in the relay, which is to act first; **provisional**, neither chosen by a measurement
- How long `@xmoq/msf` gives the Joining FETCH for a catalog before it reads on with the subscription alone: 15 seconds (`DEFAULT_FETCH_TIMEOUT_MS`), **provisional**. Longer than the own relay gives the publisher it asks (5 seconds for the answer, 5 for the fetch stream). **Since 2026-10-12 `@xmoq/moqt` gives the FETCH up itself when its answer has not come in 10 seconds** (`answerTimeoutMs`), so the 15 seconds are reached only by a FETCH that was answered and whose stream does not end. Not chosen by a measurement
- How the `namespace` of a catalog, a string, becomes a Track Namespace, a tuple: **provisional**, split at `/` with empty fields dropped, as `moqlivemock` does. MSF-01 has no rule (its §11.1.2 is for URLs), and a field that holds a `/` cannot be written this way ([`packages/msf/README.md`](../packages/msf/README.md))
- How long the catalog publisher of `@xmoq/msf` waits, when it ends, for a subscriber to take the last catalog: 3 seconds (`endTimeoutMs`), **provisional**; then the stream is reset and PUBLISH_DONE is sent
- How long a publisher that served a FETCH to its end waits for the subscriber to end its direction of the request stream before it finishes its own: 3 seconds, **provisional**, in the relay, the Rust client and `@xmoq/moqt`. Shorter loses more often to a subscriber that takes the FIN for a failure (`moqtransport` 0.14.0); longer holds the stream longer for one that waits for the FIN (the `moqtail` relay 0.15.0). Not measured
- The most bytes of an MSF catalog a reader parses: 1 MiB in `@xmoq/msf` (`DEFAULT_MAX_CATALOG_LENGTH`), **provisional**. The draft sets no size. The number is from the one catalog of another implementation that was seen, 2,715 bytes for 13 tracks; no catalog with initialization data was seen
- Messages the relay does not accept, and what each waits for: REQUEST_UPDATE for anything but the SUBGROUP_DELIVERY_TIMEOUT of a subscription (the late-join decision above), GOAWAY (hosting), SUBSCRIBE_NAMESPACE (rooms are found through the Durable Object, decided on 2026-10-11, so nothing waits for it now), TRACK_STATUS (no use planned)
- Authorization token handling in MoQ messages (§10.2.2 of the draft). Decided on 2026-10-11: **a token in every request, checked by the relay with a key it shares with the room (HMAC), to begin with; a signature the relay checks with a public key when there is more than one relay.** A connection is given the token once, and the library puts it into every request and into SETUP; the relay with a key checks SETUP too and closes a session without one. Aliases come later; until then a relay that keeps none treats them as the draft says ([implementation notes](moqt-implementation-notes.md)). WHY a request: the room's namespace is the unit, and the relay keeps no state. WHY NOT SETUP alone: the token would have to speak for the whole session. WHY NOT a signature now: more on the room's side, for a need that comes with a second relay. WHY NOT CAT or JWT: a library, for nothing this needs yet. Without a key the relay checks nothing. **Still undecided**: who may get a token (the room of the spike gives one to anyone), how long one lives, and what happens to a subscription whose token has expired ([record](experiments/2026-10-11-authorization-tokens-of-a-room-checked-by-the-relay.md))
- Latency goal, room size, expected concurrent rooms: **undecided**
- Supported browsers after Phase 1 (Phase 1 is Chromium on desktop, see the [roadmap](roadmap.md)), operating systems, Ghostty versions: **undecided**
- CLI decoder/audio backend. Decided on 2026-10-12 for the sound's decoder: **the one in Rust alone (`opus-decoder`)**. WHY: the terminal client then builds with no C compiler and no cmake, which neither `mise.toml` nor CI has, and an eighth of a core for one track is enough. WHY NOT libopus: it is the reference and takes a sixtieth of the CPU time, at the cost of those tools in every build; to be taken up again if the Rust decoder, which has one release, turns out wrong or too slow. Still **undecided**: the video decoder (the spikes use the `openh264` crate; what its build needs was not looked at for this row) and the output (`cpal` in the spikes). For sound, two decoders ran in a spike with `cpal` as the output, without an underrun and with the same samples: `opus` 0.4.0 (libopus, built with a C compiler and cmake, which neither `mise.toml` nor CI has) and `opus-decoder` 0.1.1 (Rust alone, one release in 2026-03, about 60 times the CPU time, an eighth of a core for one track). On Linux `cpal` needs the ALSA headers. Nobody listened ([record](experiments/2026-10-12-opus-decoded-and-played-with-rust-crates.md)). For video the spikes use `openh264`
- Chat via MoQ Track or other channel, persistence and ordering. Decided on 2026-10-12: **the room's WebSocket for now, as a provisional form; the aim is to take the WebSocket away and carry everything over QUIC**. WHY the WebSocket for now: it gives every page one order and keeps what was said, which the tracks tried below do not. WHY NOT the tracks now: one order for all needs something that sets it, and the piece meant for that, a subscriber next to the relay that gathers the chat and publishes it again as one track (the first of the internal subscribers, before a recorder or a transcriber), is built once the base is whole; it is not being worked on. Still **undecided**: persistence and ordering once the chat is on QUIC. Tried on one machine ([record](experiments/2026-10-12-chat-as-moq-tracks-beside-the-rooms-websocket.md)): a track for every participant through the own relay delivers every line as fast as the room's WebSocket and gives a late joiner what was said, but the pages do not show the lines in one order, the history lasts only while each participant stays, and finding the participants needs SUBSCRIBE_NAMESPACE, which the relay does not have. The room's WebSocket gives one order and keeps the last 50 lines
- Relay and Durable Objects integration. Decided on 2026-10-11: **the publishing page registers with the room where its broadcast is (the relay's URL, the certificate hash, the namespace), and the registration holds only while the page's WebSocket to the room lives**; a viewer asks the room. WHY: nothing is added to the relay, and a broadcast that has gone does not stay registered. WHY NOT the relay telling the room: it is right about what is being published, but the relay needs an HTTP client, a secret and a mapping of namespaces to rooms, which waits for authorization and hosting. WHY NOT the room asking the relay, or discovery through SUBSCRIBE_NAMESPACE: the relay has neither an HTTP endpoint nor SUBSCRIBE_NAMESPACE. Tried in Miniflare only ([record](experiments/2026-10-11-durable-objects-room.md)); the room believes the page (no authentication yet), and nothing ran on Cloudflare. Still **to design**: what the relay and the room say to each other once there is authorization, and everything about hosting. With everything on QUIC (the chat's row above) the room's duties move: a Durable Object can neither accept nor open a WebTransport session (as the chat's record has it; Cloudflare's documents were not read again for this row), so where the room lives is part of that question
- Authentication/SSO, authorization, privacy and moderation: **undecided**
- Hosting, TLS, monitoring and operating costs: **undecided**
- Recording, replay, captions and accessibility: **not discussed**
- SNS event integration contract: **not discussed**
- Bidirectional requirements: **future possibility**
- OSS license and contribution rules: **undecided**

When an item is decided, remove it from this list. Record context, alternatives, decision, consequences and evidence where the decision takes effect: as WHY / WHY NOT comments in the code, or in the document that covers the topic. Decisions are not collected in a separate place.
