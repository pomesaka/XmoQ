# MoQ Transport implementation notes

Rulings of the Rust (`crates/moqt`) and TypeScript (`packages/moqt`) implementations, and of the relay, that no test can hold: choices of design, what neither implementation does yet, observations of other implementations and of the environment, and what depends on time or load. The rulings that a test can hold, where `draft-ietf-moq-transport-18` is silent, contradicts itself or leaves a choice, are in the shared tables of [`fixtures/`](../fixtures/) that both implementations read, or in a table-driven test of the crate where one implementation has the rule, with a comment that gives the draft's section at the code; the list of tables is in `fixtures/`.

Items marked **provisional** depend on an [open question](open-questions.md). A limit named as `peer-requests` is a row of [`fixtures/limits/limits.tsv`](../fixtures/limits/README.md), where its value is.

## Where the two implementations differ

The shared tables hold what the two have in common; these differences are left out of them.

- After a refused REQUEST_UPDATE, the Rust session still takes the SUBSCRIBE as open until the PUBLISH_DONE that follows, where `@xmoq/moqt` sends both at once, so a Joining FETCH behind them gets `INVALID_JOINING_REQUEST_ID` from it (§10.9.1, §10.12.2).
- The REQUEST_UPDATEs that wait for the answer to a FETCH of the peer are at most `held-fetch-updates` in `@xmoq/moqt` and the relay, and one more refuses the FETCH with `EXCESSIVE_LOAD`; the Rust session leaves the limit to its caller (§10.9).
- After the FETCH_OK, a REQUEST_OK that does not fit in what waits to be written on the request stream is not dropped by the Rust publishers: the FETCH ends with `EXCESSIVE_LOAD` (§10.9).
- Where this endpoint reset its direction of the request stream of a FETCH, `@xmoq/moqt` forgets the request, so a REQUEST_UPDATE that crossed the reset uses up no Request ID, and the relay does the same but where it resets an answered FETCH whose stream it was passing on or writing; which of the two the relay's resets should be is an [open question](open-questions.md) (§10.1).
- A Rust client that served a FETCH stops the subscriber's direction of the request stream with `CANCELLED` when it finishes its own, and at once beyond `MAX_LINGERING` such FETCH requests, where `@xmoq/moqt` stops it `peerStreamEndTimeoutMs` after it finished its own (§5.2).
- Where the fetch stream of a served FETCH cannot be written, the Rust client resets the request stream with `INTERNAL_ERROR` and the fetch stream with `CANCELLED`, where `@xmoq/moqt` takes the code of the fetch stream from its application (§5.2).
- The time for the peer's SETUP (`setup-wait-ms`) is counted in `@xmoq/moqt` from when it begins to connect its session, in the Rust clients from when their own SETUP was written, and in the relay from when it took the session (§3.5).
- A request that `@xmoq/moqt` refused by its Request ID alone, and that does not say what it is in time, gets only a STOP_SENDING, since REQUEST_ERROR and a FIN were written, where the Rust implementation resets (§13.6).
- The Rust implementation decodes the Token structure of an `AUTHORIZATION_TOKEN`, and `@xmoq/moqt` keeps its bytes (§10.2.2).
- After it finishes its direction of a request of the peer (a refusal, a PUBLISH_DONE, the end of a FETCH), the relay gives the peer `HubConfig::request_close_timeout` to end its own, `@xmoq/moqt` gives it `peerStreamEndTimeoutMs`, also for a request it did not keep, and then stops that direction with `CANCELLED`, and the Rust client has no such time, the modes that answer without their session included (provisional; [record](experiments/2026-10-11-a-refused-request-whose-peer-does-not-end-its-direction.md)).

## Choices of design

- A Retry Interval that a session makes up is not randomized, which §10.6.2 allows against "synchronized retry storms": every requester that is refused at the same moment is told the same time.
- A fetch stream has no idle time: it belongs to a FETCH this endpoint made and can cancel, and a relay that fills its range from upstream may be silent for the FILL_TIMEOUT it was given (§10.2.5). A publisher that pauses inside a Group longer than the idle time of a subgroup stream (`idle-subgroup-stream-ms`) loses the rest of it, and the own publishers close a Group of one Object at once ([record](experiments/2026-10-24-a-silent-subgroup-stream-is-stopped.md)).

## Known gaps against the draft

These are requirements of draft 18 that the implementations do not meet yet, and this list is the whole of them. A `FIXME:` in the code marks the place of some of them.

**The two that could do the most harm.** The first is limited for one session and open for a sender with several sessions; the second waits for the decision on authorization.

1. **Several sessions of one sender have as many shares of what the relay gives one session, in the relay.** The relay does not count sessions by where they come from, and nothing here is authorized, so a few sessions can hold all the requests and FETCH requests the relay has open on a publisher, and all that its fetch streams keep beyond its cache; any other subscriber's request for that publisher, or a FETCH the cache could answer, is then refused with `EXCESSIVE_LOAD` ([record](experiments/2026-10-13-a-session-that-asks-for-a-new-track-again-and-again.md), [record](experiments/2026-10-13-a-fetch-from-the-relays-cache-that-is-read-slowly.md)). How often the sessions together may announce is not limited (§13.1), and each accepted announcement makes the relay ask the session for the tracks of the namespace again ([record](experiments/2026-10-13-a-session-that-announces-a-namespace-again-and-again.md)).
2. **Any client can be a further source of a track** (the two gaps on several publishers, and authorization). Without a key the relay takes a PUBLISH for a track that is being broadcast from anybody, up to the sources a track may have, and passes on each Subgroup from whichever source sends it first, so a client that sends its own Subgroups under the Group IDs to come would have them passed on in place of the publisher's (derived from the rule; not run). With a key, only a holder of a token for that namespace can.

- **Authorization token structure (§10.2.2).** The sessions of both implementations carry the token as opaque bytes, so the alias types (REGISTER, USE_ALIAS, DELETE) and their rules are enforced only by a relay with a key; the clients and a relay without a key do not. Left until authentication is decided.
- **Datagrams (§11).** Neither implementation delivers an OBJECT_DATAGRAM: it is dropped without notice, and its Object is lost. The rules on the fields of an OBJECT_DATAGRAM (§11.3.1, §11.2.1.1) are checked by neither ([record](experiments/2026-10-24-datagrams-read-by-the-browser-client.md)).
- **Connection flow control for control streams first (§11.4.2, MUST: "endpoints MUST allocate connection flow control to the control streams before allocating it to any data streams"). Met by the relay as a receiver; not as a sender, nor by the clients as receivers in the browser.** As a sender, quinn-proto 0.11.19 takes the peer's connection credit for a stream when it is written to, first come first served, and Chromium 156 was not seen to give its credit by the order of `@xmoq/moqt`'s streams ([record](experiments/2026-10-25-send-order-of-data-streams-in-chromium.md)); the Rust client leaves quinn's connection window at its default. In the browser the windows are the browser's, the API has no way to set them, and a deadlock of the kind the draft names was seen there for request streams (FIXME in `packages/moqt/src/webtransport/stream-io.ts`; [record](experiments/2026-10-10-subscription-backlog-in-chromium.md); [open questions](open-questions.md)).
- **A peer that ends a session without a `WT_CLOSE_SESSION` capsule, in Rust.** The Rust implementations report it as a close of their own, also where a `wtransport` 0.7.2 peer (the `moqtail` relay and clients) closed the QUIC connection with a Session Termination Error Code: the code is read only where the end is asked for before the library has closed the connection itself (FIXME in `crates/moqt-io/src/wt.rs`). A FIN of the CONNECT stream without a capsule (`draft-ietf-webtrans-http3-16` §6) is not told from a close of this side either; only what is printed changes.
- **Stream error codes on reserved codepoints (`draft-ietf-webtrans-http3-16` §4.4, §9.5), in Rust.** A reset or a STOP_SENDING with a codepoint of the form `0x1f * N + 0x21` is reported with a code where it should be without one (FIXME at the top of `crates/moqt-io/src/wt.rs`). A peer that maps its codes as §4.4 says never sends such a codepoint.
- **The relay takes a WebTransport session only from a peer that sends the older token in its CONNECT.** What the library wants and who sends which token are in the module doc of `crates/moqt-io/src/wt.rs`. Against `moqtail`'s relay the unchanged subscriber failed as well; the relay's side was not looked at.
- **A STOP_SENDING for a stream the peer opened that arrives before the page has taken the stream is not always reported by Chromium** ([record](experiments/2026-10-11-write-abort-in-chromium.md)). A peer that cancels a request that way, without resetting its own direction, can leave `@xmoq/moqt` with a request, or an accepted subscription, it believes open. The own relay and the `moqtail` relay reset their direction as well when they cancel (the latter by its source, not run).
- **The code of a STOP_SENDING sent from a browser on a subgroup stream was not observed.** The relay notices a STOP_SENDING on a subgroup stream when it next writes to it, and no interop run has a subscriber stop a stream the relay is still writing ([record](experiments/2026-10-10-disconnects-through-own-relay.md)).
- **`@xmoq/moqt` and `@xmoq/msf` report a failure of a handler with the global `reportError`, unguarded.** In a runtime without it the call throws, the failure is an unhandled rejection, and `end` of the catalog publisher of `@xmoq/msf` then rejects, against what it says. Not run in such a runtime.
- **A FETCH of the peer that `@xmoq/moqt` accepted and that the subscriber neither reads nor gives up holds its two streams, and its place among the FETCH requests of the peer, for the session.** Details at `packages/moqt/src/core/limits.ts`.
- **`@xmoq/moqt` remembers no Object it sent.** A viewer's Joining FETCH reaches the page only as a FETCH of the relay, which the application answers from Objects it kept itself. Where the page accepts a SUBSCRIBE without LARGEST_OBJECT, the relay refuses a viewer's Joining FETCH for that subscription with `INVALID_RANGE`.
- **Through `moqtail`'s relay, what a page answers a FETCH with does not reach the viewer.** ([record](experiments/2026-10-11-a-page-answers-a-fetch.md)). That relay sends the viewer a fetch stream that carries the Request ID of its own FETCH to the publisher, which no FETCH of the viewer has. The own relay writes the viewer's Request ID.
- **Nothing bounds how many requests of `@xmoq/moqt` wait for a stream at once, nor what is kept for a stream that is being opened.** Details at `packages/moqt/src/webtransport/stream-io.ts`.
- **A write on a stream of `@xmoq/moqt` that could not be opened closes the session, where the failure to open it did not.** Why no such write is made is in `packages/moqt/src/webtransport/stream-io.ts`.
- **A limit on the payload of an Object for one subscription holds only from its SUBSCRIBE_OK on.** An Object whose length was read while the Track Alias of its stream was unknown is under the connection's limit ([`fixtures/limits/`](../fixtures/limits/README.md)). A fetch stream is decoded by its reader, which can give a smaller limit.
- **A SUBSCRIBE that the Rust client reads before it has sent its PUBLISH gets `NOT_SUPPORTED`** (`crates/moqt-client/src/main.rs`, where the rest is, with what a subscriber behind the relay gets).
- **The Rust client in a mode that publishes leaves its peer unanswered in two waits.** The two waits, and what it answers in the others, are in `crates/moqt-client/src/main.rs`.
- **A GOAWAY with a New Session URI on a request stream (§10.4, SHOULD: "the endpoint SHOULD re-issue that specific request on a session at the specified URI").** The clients that ask a request again after one ask it on the session they have, where the URI names another: each keeps one session (FIXME in `crates/subscriber/src/kept.rs`, and in the sessions of `xmoq-chat` and `xmoq-transcriber`). The own relay sends no GOAWAY on a request stream.
- **A publisher that is one of the two clients does not look at the Subscription Filter of a SUBSCRIBE (§5.1.2, MUST NOT).** `@xmoq/moqt` hands the SUBSCRIBE of a peer to its application with the filter in it, and sends what the application writes; the Rust client in the mode that serves a SUBSCRIBE sends every Object from the next one on. The own relay asks a publisher without a filter, so neither is asked for one behind it.
- **The application gets Immutable Properties as one property with its bytes.** Both sessions, and the readers of LOC in both implementations, look inside them for what they read. Any other reader of a property above the session has to open them itself (§12.7, MUST).
- **A Joining FETCH for a subscription that a PUBLISH made is refused (§5.1, §10.12.2).** §5.1 gives such a subscription a Joining Location, and the change log of draft 18 has "Allow joining FETCH for PUBLISH and REQUEST_UPDATE with forward=1"; the Rust session takes only a SUBSCRIBE of the peer, answers `INVALID_JOINING_REQUEST_ID` for the rest, and sends no Joining FETCH that names a PUBLISH of the peer (FIXME in `crates/moqt/src/session/peer.rs`). It is met when a viewer that got a track by PUBLISH sends a Joining FETCH to the publisher that published it: of the own clients, the browser publisher of `interop/page.ts` and the Rust client send PUBLISH, and the relay sends none.
- **The Forward State of the subscription a Joining FETCH names is not looked at by the session (§10.12.2, MUST).** "A Joining Fetch is only permitted when the associated subscription has Forward State 1; otherwise the publisher MUST respond with a REQUEST_ERROR with error code INVALID_RANGE." Whoever answers the FETCH has to check it: the relay does; the Rust client refuses every Joining FETCH with `NOT_SUPPORTED`, and `@xmoq/moqt` refuses one read before its SUBSCRIBE with the same code by itself.
- **NEW_GROUP_REQUEST (§10.2.13), DYNAMIC_GROUPS (§12.6). Met in part.** The relay passes the request on. `@xmoq/moqt` refuses every PUBLISH of the peer with `NOT_SUPPORTED`, so a page has no subscription a PUBLISH made to ask for; the Rust session accepts one and can ask, and only the relay does.
- **REQUEST_UPDATE** is applied for one thing: the SUBGROUP_DELIVERY_TIMEOUT of a subscription, in the relay. Every other update of a subscription is refused, so its Forward State, filter and priority cannot change, and an update of a FETCH is accepted and changes nothing; the two clients send one for a SUBSCRIBE of their own, none for a FETCH or a PUBLISH_NAMESPACE. **A REQUEST_UPDATE that gets no answer leaves the subscription as it is**: after the time a request gets, the browser client rejects the call and the Rust client reports it, neither ends the subscription, and an answer that comes later is still read.
- **A Message Parameter in a REQUEST_UPDATE on the stream of a request that is over is not looked at (§10.2.1, MUST).** After a refusal, and after the answer to a FETCH has ended, neither implementation looks, where §10.2.1 has "If it appears in some other type of message, the receiving endpoint MUST close the connection with a PROTOCOL_VIOLATION" without an exception for a request that is over. Closing the session there would take every other track of that session along.

The relay (`crates/relay-core`, `crates/relay`) adds these:

- **Several publishers of one track (§9.3, §9.5). The MUSTs are met in part.** Five things are not done, each against one of the MUSTs of §9.3 and §9.5 (listed in the crate doc of `crates/relay-core/src/lib.rs`): no more publishers are asked than the track has room for; a publisher passed over for the requests the relay has open on it is not asked when one of them ends; one that was asked and did not become a source is not asked again until it announces anew; a PUBLISH_NAMESPACE does not get its publisher asked for a track that has a source and no subscriber; and a SUBSCRIBE without `RENDEZVOUS_TIMEOUT` for a track whose sources all drain gets `DOES_NOT_EXIST`. No PUBLISH is forwarded to a subscriber, where §9.5 has "Relays MUST forward PUBLISH_NAMESPACE or PUBLISH messages to all matching subscribers".
- **Sources of a track that do not send the same thing (§9.1, §2.4.2, §9.3).** Of the Forwarding Preference, Subgroup ID, Priority and Payload that §9.1 has compared for a duplicate Object, the relay compares the Priority alone, for a Subgroup it remembers and where it can tell the priority, and removes duplicates by Subgroup and not by Object. Runs with two publishers were done with publishers that send the same Object under the same Group ID only.
- **A session that has as many timers running as it may gets the next one at once.** A timer beyond that limit is not started and is reported as run out at once, so a wait for the last streams after PUBLISH_DONE ends as soon as it begins, an early stream is dropped at once, and a request the relay sent counts as unanswered at once. The timers that give a session an announcement back and that send an announcement to the parent again are started all the same (`crates/relay/src/hub.rs`).
- **A stream of a subscription that comes after a new subscription took its Track Alias (§10.11, §11.1).** A stream that a PUBLISH_DONE counted and that is still on its way when a new subscription takes the alias is taken for one of the new subscription: its Objects go out under it, its end counts towards the new subscription's Stream Count, and it is read with the new subscription's DEFAULT PUBLISHER PRIORITY, which ends the track as malformed where the two defaults differ for a Subgroup the relay remembers. The same happens the other way round, with a stream of the new subscription read before the PUBLISH_DONE of the first, and the draft gives a receiver no way to tell the streams apart.
- **Streams a publisher sent ahead for a PUBLISH that is refused, under the Track Alias of a subscription that drains (§5.1, §10.10, §10.11).** When such a PUBLISH is refused, the alias means the draining subscription again, and a stream sent ahead for it is taken for one of the draining subscription: its Objects go to that track's subscribers, also where the refused PUBLISH was for another track, and its end counts towards the Stream Count, which can then be reached before the counted streams have ended. The draft gives a receiver no way to tell the two apart; a refused SUBSCRIBE_OK leaves the same behind.
- **A Subgroup without a Publisher Priority reaches the subscribers under the default of another source (§11.4.2, §12.4).** The relay passes the header on as it came, and the subscribers have the Track Properties of the source that came last (§2.5), so with two sources whose defaults differ, a Subgroup of the first is read with the default of the other.
- **Two DEFAULT PUBLISHER PRIORITY properties in one message are not refused (§12.4).** The relay takes the first where it compares priorities, and passes both on.
- **A Publisher Priority is compared only for a Subgroup the relay remembers, and not where it cannot tell the default (§2.4.2).** A Subgroup that comes again after the relay has forgotten it, and a stream without a priority in its header under a Track Alias whose subscription drains, are not compared; after the latter, the Subgroup is compared again only once it has been taken from a stream whose priority is known. All of that is on the side of not finding a difference.
- **The fourth example of a malformed track is looked for across streams only in the Group the cache keeps (§2.4.2).** An Object past the End of Group is found across streams only for the Group the cache keeps and has not given up, and otherwise within one subgroup stream. The seventh, another Forwarding Preference than before, cannot come about, since the relay reads no datagrams.
- **Objects past the end of a Subgroup, and a Subgroup on two streams that both end with a FIN with different last Objects, where the relay does not read the second stream (§2.4.2, the second and third example of a malformed track).** The relay finds both only on a second stream of the same source for a Subgroup it delivered whole. Where it does not look is listed in `crates/relay-core/src/lib.rs`.
- **A second stream for a Subgroup the relay has forgotten (§2.2).** It passes, so the subscribers can get a Subgroup on two streams of which neither was reset, which §2.2 has as MUST NOT. The relay forgets past `MAX_REMEMBERED_SUBGROUPS` Subgroups over the `MAX_REMEMBERED_GROUPS` newest Groups of a track, which a publisher that sends every Object as a Subgroup of its own reaches; with one source per track it also takes a publisher that sends a Subgroup twice, and once a track has had several sources only the limit of `MAX_REMEMBERED_SUBGROUPS` is left.
- **Which of two open streams for a Subgroup the source goes on with (§11.4).** The relay keeps the one whose header it read later, so when a source resets a stream, sends the Subgroup again, and the header of the first stream is read after that of the second, the relay keeps the first, which ends with the reset, and the Subgroup is lost for every subscriber (§11.4.3). Whether this happens with real peers was not looked at.
- **A Subgroup that a subscriber stopped reaches it again on another stream of its source (§11.4.3).** Where the Subgroup is taken anew from another stream of the source, a subscription whose stream was stopped with `STOP_SENDING` gets one again, against "SHOULD NOT attempt to open a new stream to deliver additional Objects in that Subgroup"; a stop that comes after the last Object is not marked, since the relay sees it when it next writes. Not run with a peer that sends `STOP_SENDING` on a subgroup stream.
- **A SUBSCRIBE whose End Group is over already, or whose filter has no Location to start at, is accepted (§5.1.2, SHOULD).** The relay answers SUBSCRIBE_OK where §5.1.2 has REQUEST_ERROR `INVALID_RANGE`, and sends nothing where no Location follows the Largest Object; it does not know that an End Group is published whole, and a Group at or before the End Group that reaches it late is still sent. The MUST NOT of that paragraph is kept.
- **A subscription is not ended when the End Group of its filter has passed.** No PUBLISH_DONE with `SUBSCRIPTION_ENDED` (0x3) is sent; the subscription stays until the subscriber ends it or the track does. §9.4 does not ask it of a relay.
- **Delivery timeouts (§8, §10.2.3, §10.2.4). Met in part.** The relay acts on the `SUBGROUP_DELIVERY_TIMEOUT` of a SUBSCRIBE and of a REQUEST_UPDATE, and its own time for the subscribers that set none, off unless it is told, leaves §8's "if none of the timeouts are set to a non-zero value, all Objects ... are delivered" for them ([record](experiments/2026-10-12-a-page-behind-a-link-with-too-little-room-and-a-relay-that-gives-groups-up.md), [record](experiments/2026-10-12-the-relay-follows-the-delivery-timeout-a-viewer-asks-for.md)). **Not met** (the FIXME at the top of `crates/relay-core/src/lib.rs`): the publisher's Track Properties `SUBGROUP_DELIVERY_TIMEOUT` and `OBJECT_DELIVERY_TIMEOUT` are not read, `OBJECT_DELIVERY_TIMEOUT` in a SUBSCRIBE or a REQUEST_UPDATE is accepted and not acted on, and every Object goes to the send window of the connection as it comes, so "SHOULD minimize the amount of data buffered at the underlying transport layer" is not followed. The two clients act on neither.
- **No rate limit on subscription requests (§13.1, SHOULD).** The relay has none for the subscription requests of a subscriber; a session that does not end its requests is stopped by the limit on those it may have open, and a subscriber that asks for a new track name each time has the relay send one SUBSCRIBE to every session that announced a namespace of the track. With four of them the Objects of the sessions beside it were not late, and more than four publishers and a machine with one core were not run ([record](experiments/2026-10-13-a-session-that-asks-for-a-new-track-again-and-again.md)).
- **Retry Interval (§10.6.2).** 0, which reads as "do not retry", is given with `DUPLICATE_SUBSCRIPTION` where a retry could succeed a moment later (a PUBLISH read before the publisher's own PUBLISH_DONE for the track, a viewer that asks anew before it was sent PUBLISH_DONE), and the values are fixed numbers, not randomized. The relay does not honour a Retry Interval either: a publisher that declined a track is asked for it at once when it announces the namespace anew, also after 0, and where the last refusal ended a track that had no source, the next subscriber has every matching publisher asked again at once. Where several publishers were asked, the subscribers get the Retry Interval of the answer that came last.
- **`DUPLICATE_SUBSCRIPTION` from a publisher that sends no PUBLISH (§5.1, §10.6.2).** The code is also the answer of a publisher that still holds a subscription in the same role, for instance one the relay cancelled a moment ago; the relay cannot tell this from an announced PUBLISH and waits, and the subscribers that wait get `TIMEOUT` where asking the publisher again would have got them the track (FIXME in `crates/relay-core/src/routing.rs`).
- **Priorities (§7.2, §7.3). Met in part.** Each stream the relay opens to a subscriber gets a priority of the transport from the SUBSCRIBER_PRIORITY and the stream's Publisher Priority, §7.2's first two steps, and not the third and fourth, the Group by the GROUP_ORDER and the lowest Subgroup ID (WHY NOT in `crates/relay-core/src/lib.rs`); a SUBSCRIBER_PRIORITY in a REQUEST_UPDATE of a subscription is refused, and the clients give the transport no priority. A priority does not reach the first bytes of every stream, which `web-transport-quinn` 0.13.2 writes with the highest priority there is; what waits beyond the send window of the connection ([record](experiments/2026-10-15-what-the-sound-waits-behind-on-a-narrow-link.md)); or the relay's limit on what waits for one subscriber, which is one for the session ([record](experiments/2026-10-14-three-minutes-behind-a-narrow-link-and-the-relays-limit-for-one-viewer.md)).
- **Not verified against a real connection: what the relay does when it gives a fetch stream up before it is open.** What is tested, and on which connections, is in `crates/relay/src/hub.rs`.
- **The relay does not look into a fetch stream it passes on (§2.4.2, §11.4).** It does not find what breaks the object model there, so a publisher's malformed fetch stream reaches the subscriber as it is, where §2.4.2 has "If a relay detects a Malformed Track, it MUST immediately terminate downstream subscriptions with PUBLISH_DONE and reset any fetch streams with Status Code MALFORMED_TRACK". A stream that ends with a FIN inside an Object is passed on with its FIN as well, and nothing of a fetch stream raises the largest Location the relay knows for the track.
- **The relay leaves the range of a FETCH to the publisher (§10.12.3, §10.13).** For a FETCH it asks a publisher for, whether the range starts after the largest Object, where it is cut when it ends beyond it, and the order of the Groups are what the publisher answers; the relay compares neither the answer nor the stream with what it knows of the track. The `moqtail` test client as a publisher answers some of these otherwise than the draft has them ([record](experiments/2026-10-11-standalone-fetch-through-own-relay.md)).
- **A FETCH_OK with a Mandatory Track Property that comes after the fetch stream was passed on whole resets nothing (§2.5.1).** "If the relay has already forwarded data on a fetch stream, it MUST reset the stream": when the publisher's stream ended with a FIN before its FETCH_OK came, there is no stream left to reset, and the subscriber gets REQUEST_ERROR `UNSUPPORTED_EXTENSION` after a fetch stream that looked complete.
- **The relay's cache is the latest Group of a track, and nothing a FETCH brought (§9.1).** A FETCH for anything else is asked of a publisher, also for Objects that passed through the relay a moment ago, and the answer a publisher gives to a FETCH the relay passes on is not cached.
- **Objects that come again at the relay (§9.1, §2.4.2, §12.9).** *One that comes with other content, and a Group that contradicts itself (MUST)*, are found only in the latest Group of the track as far as the cache has it: not in an earlier Group, a Group the cache gave up, a track that is not cached at all, or a fetch stream the relay passes on. *One that comes where the relay recorded that none exists is forwarded*, against "A caching relay SHOULD NOT cache or forward the Object in this case" (§9.1).
- **The end of a track at the relay (§2.4.2, §10.13).** The end that a FETCH says is taken from its FETCH_OK and not from its fetch stream, and where a larger Object or another final Object is known, that FETCH alone is refused with `MALFORMED_TRACK` and the track goes on, where §2.4.2 would have the track malformed. FETCH_OK from the relay's cache has End Of Track 0 where no Object with the status END_OF_TRACK came and no publisher's FETCH_OK gave the final Object, also after PUBLISH_DONE with TRACK_ENDED; answering with 1 needs the relay to keep a track that has ended, which `crates/relay-core/src/cache.rs` has as not done.
- **A Descending FETCH with FILL_TIMEOUT 0 whose range reaches beyond the Group the relay's cache keeps gets no Object (§10.2.5).** The whole range is one End of Unknown Range indicator, where an Ascending one gets the cache's Objects (WHY NOT in `crates/relay-core/src/fetch.rs`). §11.4.4.2 does not say where an indicator begins in a Descending stream.
- **A Joining FETCH at the relay.**
  - **A Joining FETCH that has no FETCH_OK yet ends when its subscription does (§10.12.2).** The draft says nothing of a subscription that ends; the relay refuses the FETCH with `INVALID_JOINING_REQUEST_ID` and cancels what it asked of the publisher, whatever ended the subscription, though only a cancel by the subscriber is the subscriber's wish. A viewer whose publisher ends the track, or that is slow at the moment it joins, loses the Groups before as well, if the publisher has not answered the FETCH by then.
  - **What a Joining FETCH and a subscription that starts at or before the Joining Location deliver can overlap.** With no filter, or an `AbsoluteStart` or `AbsoluteRange` filter whose Start Location is not after the Joining Location, an Object at or before the Joining Location that reaches the relay after the SUBSCRIBE, on a stream that was behind, goes out on the subscription and is in the fetch stream as well; the draft states "contiguous and non-overlapping" for no filter in particular. The `moqtail` relay 0.15.0 showed an overlap of another origin ([record](experiments/2026-10-11-moqtail-relay-fetch.md), a).
  - **A Joining FETCH can miss Objects where a track has several sources (§10.12.2.1).** The Joining Location is the largest Location the relay knows of the track from any source, and the first source is asked first, so a publisher that is behind and answers with what it has leaves what lies between its largest Object and the Joining Location out of both the fetch stream and the subscription. Which publisher a FETCH should go to where several publish a track is not decided; not run against real publishers.
  - **A Joining FETCH for a SUBSCRIBE with Forward State 0 that the relay has not answered is held, and refused when the SUBSCRIBE is accepted (§10.12.2).** The Forward State is in the SUBSCRIBE, so `INVALID_RANGE` could be said at once; the relay says it where it works the range out.
- **The relay passes an Object on only once it is whole (§5.1: "A publisher SHOULD begin sending incomplete objects when available").** A subscriber gets an Object that is under way when its last byte has reached the relay, and a FETCH whose range has one is held until then.
- **The publisher does not learn the priority of the relay's FETCH.** The relay's FETCH names no SUBSCRIBER_PRIORITY, and a REQUEST_UPDATE for a FETCH is answered by the relay and moves only the fetch stream to the subscriber, so where the publisher's link to the relay is the narrow one, the publisher orders the relay's fetch streams by its own defaults.
- **Messages the relay does not implement (§4, MUST: "Relays MUST implement all MOQT messages defined in this document, as well as processing rules described in Section 9").** SUBSCRIBE_TRACKS and datagrams are refused or not read, SUBSCRIBE_NAMESPACE is served without a REQUEST_UPDATE of it, and REQUEST_UPDATE is applied for one parameter of a subscription, passes a NEW_GROUP_REQUEST on, and is refused for the others.
- **Authorization (§9.4, §9.5).** Off unless the relay is given a key; with one, a subscription that was allowed goes on after its token has expired (the FIXME in `crates/relay/src/auth.rs`), and a publisher's `UNAUTHORIZED`, `MALFORMED_AUTH_TOKEN` or `EXPIRED_AUTH_TOKEN` to the relay's SUBSCRIBE reaches the subscribers as it is. Any session can announce any namespace, the one with no fields included, which matches every track, and with several sources of a track any session can become a source of any track that has one by sending PUBLISH for it, and then end the track for every subscriber, take its Subgroups, keep the other sources from being heard, or raise its largest Location. Any session can also ask any publisher for a FETCH of any track.

## Other observations about the draft

- **The first Object of a fetch stream after an End of Range indicator (§11.4.4.1, §11.4.4.2).** §11.4.4.1 has "The first Object MUST include a Group ID Delta and Object ID Delta, and these values are the absolute Group ID and Object ID", and §11.4.4.2 gives the prior Group ID and Object ID after an indicator without a condition; XmoQ follows §11.4.4.2, the later and more specific section, so such an Object may take its IDs from the indicator and a Group ID Delta it carries counts from the indicator's Group. **Provisional** until bytes of another implementation are seen.
- **The two fields of an End of Range indicator (§11.4.4, §11.4.4.2).** The figure of §11.4.4 names them Group ID Delta and Object ID Delta, and §11.4.4.2 gives "this Location"; XmoQ reads and writes a Location, **provisional** until bytes of another implementation are seen ([record](experiments/2026-10-11-moqtail-relay-fetch.md)).
- **Where an End of Unknown Range indicator stands in a Descending fetch (§11.4.4.2).** "this Location" is not said to be the near or the far end there; a publisher writes the far end, after the Objects (**provisional**), and the relay, where it answers a FETCH with FILL_TIMEOUT 0 itself, writes one indicator at the last Location of the range. No bytes of another implementation with an indicator in a Descending fetch were seen.
- **A FETCH without FILL_TIMEOUT (§10.2.5).** The requester gives it the base time for an answer alone, though "the relay waits for an implementation specific duration" the requester cannot know. Giving up a request the peer does not answer was run for a SUBSCRIBE, a PUBLISH and a FETCH, not for a PUBLISH_NAMESPACE ([record](experiments/2026-10-12-a-request-that-is-not-answered-is-given-up.md)).
- **A browser page that is closed without `close()` ends differently at the relay by browser** (observed, [record of browser support](experiments/2026-10-10-browser-support-webtransport-webcodecs.md)). With Chromium 156 the relay sees the session end first, as a close by the peer with code 0; with the Firefox 157.0 build of Playwright it sees the page's control stream end first, and closes the session itself with `PROTOCOL_VIOLATION`, which is what §3.3 asks for. The subscribers of a track that page published get PUBLISH_DONE `TRACK_ENDED` with the Stream Count in both cases, and the relay's grace for a STOP_SENDING on its own control stream does not apply here.
- The second example in §11.6 does not add up (a 14-byte value under `Length = 21`). It is not used as a test fixture.
- **§10.2.2 has a message rejected with a code that only ends sessions.** "The receiver of a message referencing an Alias that is not currently registered MUST reject the message with UNKNOWN_AUTH_TOKEN_ALIAS", where the code of that name is among the Session Termination Error Codes (§3.5, 0x17) and not among the codes of REQUEST_ERROR (§10.6). **XmoQ closes the session with 0x17**: the one code of that name there is, which a peer can read.
- **§12.9 calls malformed what §2.1 and §9.1 allow.** §2.1 and §9.1 let an Object known to exist transition to not existing, and §12.9 has a track malformed when "An endpoint receives an Object with a Prior Object ID Gap covering an Object it previously received", which is such a transition, said with the property. XmoQ follows each where it applies: the gap is malformed, a status in the place of an Object is not (`crates/relay-core/src/cache.rs`).
- **§2.1 says "not malformed" where §2.4.2 and §12.9 say malformed.** §2.1 has an Object that comes after the endpoint recorded that it does not exist as "not a protocol error and the Track is not malformed", with no condition, where §2.4.2 ("An Object is received in a Group whose Object ID is larger than the final Object in the Group") and §12.9 ("An endpoint receives an Object with an Object ID within a previously communicated gap") have such Objects make the track malformed. Where XmoQ puts the border is in `crates/relay-core/src/cache.rs`.
- **Prior Object ID Gap has a type from the range §2.5 leaves to applications.** §2.5 reserves 0x38 to 0x3F (1-byte encoding) for application-specific use, and §12.9 defines Prior Object ID Gap with the type 0x3E, and §12.8 Prior Group ID Gap with 0x3C. XmoQ reads 0x3E as §12.9 has it, so an application that uses 0x3E for something of its own makes its track malformed or leaves holes in what the relay's cache answers.
- `moqtail` 0.15.0 clients omit `MOQT_IMPLEMENTATION` in SETUP (§10.3.1.5 is a SHOULD); its relay sends it. The relay's control-message log prints the raw bytes received and sent, checked in `libs/moqtail-rs/src/transport/control_stream_handler.rs` at tag `relay@0.15.0`.
- **`moqtail` 0.15.0 keeps the prior Group ID and Object ID of the last Object after an End of Range indicator on a fetch stream**, where §11.4.4.2 has those of the indicator (`src/model/data/fetch_object.rs`: "Returns None for EndOfRange markers — they MUST NOT update prior state"). For the same reason it takes an Object that follows an indicator, with no Object before, for the first of the stream, whose Group ID and Object ID have to be there and are absolute; an Object that follows an indicator and leaves out its Group ID or Object ID is read as another Object by the two. Not run: no stream with an indicator was obtained.
- **`moqtail` 0.15.0 has a third Group Order, `Original` = 0x0** (`src/model/control/constant.rs`), and reads a GROUP_ORDER of 0 as it, where §10.2.8 has "Ascending (0x1) or Descending (0x2)" and a `PROTOCOL_VIOLATION` for any other value. XmoQ closes the session for 0.
- **The `moqtail` relay 0.15.0 refuses a Relative Joining FETCH whose Joining Start is larger than the Group of the Joining Location** (its log: "Joining fetch start location is larger than the track's largest location"). §10.12.2.1 has the Start Location "{Joining Location.Group - Joining Start, 0}" and does not say what it is when that cannot be subtracted; the own relay starts at Group 0.
- **The `moqtail` relay 0.15.0 answers a FETCH in five ways draft 18 does not have** ([record](experiments/2026-10-11-moqtail-relay-fetch.md), one run each, with moqtail's test client as the publisher). The own relay is to follow the draft in each.

  | | Case | Draft 18 | The `moqtail` relay |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | a | The range of a Joining FETCH | Ends at the Joining Location, the Largest Location of the SUBSCRIBE_OK (§5.1, §10.12.2.1) | Counted from the Largest Location the relay itself has seen of the track when the FETCH arrives, which is {0, 0} until an Object has passed through it, while the SUBSCRIBE_OK it sends down carries the publisher's LARGEST_OBJECT (`apps/relay/src/server/message_handlers/fetch_handler.rs` at tag `relay@0.15.0`). **So a page gets no MSF catalog through this relay before the catalog is published anew** ([record](experiments/2026-10-25-moqtail-relay-joining-fetch-of-a-catalog.md)) |
  | b | End Location {G, 0} | "the entire group is requested" (§10.12.1) | Nothing of Group G is sent |
  | c | Start Location beyond the largest Object | REQUEST_ERROR `INVALID_RANGE` (§10.12.3, MUST) | FETCH_OK with the End Location that was asked for, and a stream without Objects |
  | d | A Joining FETCH whose Joining Request ID is no subscription | `INVALID_JOINING_REQUEST_ID` (§10.12.2) | REQUEST_ERROR `DOES_NOT_EXIST`, "Track does not exist" |
  | e | GROUP_ORDER Descending | "A publisher MUST send fetched groups in the requested group order" (§10.12.3) | The first Group only, and the stream stays open |

  b and e are what `moqtail`'s test client does as the publisher: it answered the same way when the own relay passed the FETCH on to it ([record](experiments/2026-10-11-standalone-fetch-through-own-relay.md), runs 6 and 7). For c it is not known whether the relay or the test client behind it decides.

## Observations of other implementations and of the browser

- `moqtail` 0.15.0 answers `LARGEST_OBJECT` {0, 0} in TRACK_STATUS_OK for a track it has before any Object came, and puts no Track Properties in it (§10.14; capture P of `fixtures/moqt-18/`).
- The `moqtail` relay 0.15.0 treats a Standalone FETCH whose End Location is before its Start Location as a protocol violation (`resolve_standalone_fetch_range` in its `fetch_handler.rs`), where §10.12.3 names no consequence and XmoQ answers `INVALID_RANGE`.
- The relay and libraries of `moqtail` 0.15.0 and `moqtransport` 0.14.0 send a Retry Interval of 0 in every REQUEST_ERROR they make (§10.6.2).
- `moqtail` 0.15.0 (`model/data/fetch_object.rs`) and `Eyevinn/moqtransport` v0.14.0 (`internal/wire2/fetch_object.go`, `readEndOfRange`; source read, not run) read the two fields of an End of Range indicator as a Location, and `moqtail` 0.15.0 reads no Object Payload Length after them (§11.4.4.2). Through `moqtail`'s relay 0.15.0, a fetch stream with an indicator at {6, 0} and then Groups 7 to 9 came out as its Objects alone, as Groups 0 to 2, the relay forwarding the upstream Objects without the indicators ([record](experiments/2026-10-24-chat-gatherer.md)).
- `Eyevinn/moqtransport` v0.14.0 takes the Subgroup ID and Priority of an Object after an End of Range indicator from the last Object, as XmoQ does (§11.4.4.2; `internal/wire2/fetch_object.go`, source read, not run).
- `moqtail` 0.15.0 refuses a DEFAULT PUBLISHER PRIORITY above 255 as a protocol violation when it decodes it, as XmoQ does (§12.4; `src/model/property/track_property.rs`, read, not run).
- `moqtail` 0.15.0 counts the inner Types of Immutable Properties from 0 as XmoQ does, takes nesting for a `ProtocolViolation` where §12.7 has the track malformed, and does not look inside for its check of the gaps (`validate_prior_gaps` in `src/model/property/object_property.rs`). Not run against a peer that sends the property ([record](experiments/2026-10-24-immutable-properties-in-the-browser-client.md)).
- `moqtail`'s relay and test client at `relay@0.15.0` (commit dd8623d) neither send NEW_GROUP_REQUEST nor act on it, though its libraries encode the parameter and DYNAMIC_GROUPS, so the parameter is run between own parts only (§10.2.13; [record](experiments/2026-10-13-a-subscriber-asks-for-a-new-group.md)).
- moqtail-ts 0.13.0 answers a SUBSCRIBE with a SUBSCRIBE_OK that repeats the parameters of the SUBSCRIBE (`handlerSubscribe` in the npm package `moqtail` 0.13.0), so a SUBSCRIBE with FORWARD gets a SUBSCRIBE_OK with FORWARD, for which XmoQ closes the session (§10.2.1, §10.2.12). The relay's SUBSCRIBE carries no FORWARD, so a moqtail-ts publisher behind it is not closed for this ([closed while the relay's SUBSCRIBE carried FORWARD](experiments/2026-10-10-relay-namespace-routing.md), run 10; [the run that passes](experiments/2026-10-10-routing-follow-ups.md)).
- Chromium permits a peer at least 100 streams of each kind and takes no lower wish from the page (`kMinimumIncomingWebTransportStreams` in `net/quic/dedicated_web_transport_http3_client.cc`, read on 2026-10-11), so towards `@xmoq/moqt` the streams beyond those it reads, up to 100, sit below the implementation with what the peer wrote to them ([record](experiments/2026-10-11-streams-a-page-takes-from-a-peer.md)).
- Chromium 156 wraps a close code outside 32 bits and resolves `closed` with the reason as it was given (`draft-ietf-webtrans-http3-16` §6; [record](experiments/2026-10-10-webtransport-close-info-in-chromium.md)).
