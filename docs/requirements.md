# Requirements and status

**Discussed** means explicitly raised in planning; **proposed** means suggested but not confirmed; **decided** means confirmed by the project owner and not yet implemented; **implemented** means the code is in `main` and a recorded run in `docs/experiments/` exercised it; **validation required** means not proven.

| Requirement | Status |
| --- | --- |
| Own MoQ Transport implementation in Rust and TypeScript, pinned to draft 18 | Implemented for the Phase 0b scope: the codec and session in Rust and TypeScript, a client in each, and a minimal relay in Rust that routes by track and by announced namespace and takes one track from up to 4 publishers. One Object passes between every pairing of own and `moqtail` clients through either relay. Implemented since, beyond that scope (each in both implementations where it is a client's matter, and in the interop run): FETCH (Standalone and Joining); delivery timeouts that the relay acts on, and a REQUEST_UPDATE that changes them; the relay sending by Subscriber and Publisher Priority; a SUBSCRIBE the relay holds for a publisher to come (RENDEZVOUS_TIMEOUT); and limits and timeouts for a peer that does not answer, sends no SETUP, or holds nothing. TRACK_STATUS (decided on 2026-10-24, §4 MUST): the relay answers it, and both clients send it ([record](experiments/2026-10-25-track-status-through-the-relays.md)). Each is implemented in part: what of the draft is not, also within these, is listed in [implementation notes](moqt-implementation-notes.md) |
| One-to-many live broadcast | Implemented on loopback: a page's video and audio reach several viewers through the relay (`mise run demo`), and one video track reached 1,000 viewers of the Rust client on one Mac ([record](experiments/2026-10-11-one-video-track-to-many-viewers.md)) and on Linux in a VM ([record](experiments/2026-10-24-one-video-track-to-many-viewers-on-linux.md)). Validation required over real links. Decided on 2026-10-25, in two steps: **one relay serves 500 viewers of a broadcast, and relays can be added to build a broadcast of 10,000** (row "Latency, capacity"); how relays would be added is proposed ([design draft](scale-and-meetings.md)), and the own relay takes another relay as its upstream for the tracks nobody announced at it, implemented and run with one Object through two relays on one machine ([record](experiments/2026-10-25-a-relay-below-another-relay.md)); what is missing for the rest is in that draft |
| Browser publishing and viewing | Implemented for Phase 1: Chromium (Chrome, Edge) on desktop, on loopback, in the demo, with audio added. The demo also ran in Playwright's Firefox ([record](experiments/2026-10-11-demo-in-firefox-and-webkit.md)). Which other browsers are supported is undecided |
| WebCodecs and MoQ/WebTransport | Implemented in Chromium on one machine: H.264 and Opus of WebCodecs over the own MoQ Transport. Validation required on real links and in Safari |
| Media packaged as LOC, tracks described by an MSF catalog (`draft-ietf-moq-msf-01`), with the FETCH that needs | The MSF catalog is implemented: `@xmoq/msf` reads, follows and publishes a catalog track, run against `moqlivemock` in both directions; delta updates and GZIP are not. Media in LOC: the timing properties and the H.264 payload of `draft-ietf-moq-loc-04` on draft 18 are implemented in `@xmoq/loc` (the revision decided on 2026-10-11), and `@xmoq/media` receives, sends, publishes and plays a video track with them, a Group on one stream (decided on 2026-10-11); a page played the video of `moqlivemock`'s publisher, and a page encodes video in a browser that another page plays through the relay; the demo's page draws the frames (`demo/`, not a package). A relay cache of the latest Group of a track is implemented for a track that has a publisher, with the bounds decided on 2026-10-11 (8 MiB and 4,096 Objects a track, 256 MiB in all); keeping it for 30 seconds after the last publisher has gone is decided and not implemented. What is provisional is in the [open decisions](open-questions.md) |
| Audio | Implemented in its smallest form: Opus, mono, page to page through the relay and in the demo, played by the clock of the picture after one buffer (`createPlayout`; 150 ms by default, decided on 2026-10-21). A piece of sound is a Group and a stream of its own (decided on 2026-10-13). What was measured and what was not is in [the A/V demo and its limits](av-demo.md). A person found the sound smooth on 2026-10-12. Validation required: the sync was not judged by eye and ear |
| Rust relay and Rust CLI | The relay is implemented as the rows above say. A CLI is a first version: `xmoq-watch` (`mise run watch`), a viewer for the terminal that plays picture and sound by one clock, chooses a version of the video by a flag, and can come before the broadcast; run on macOS, and on Linux with no sound output ([record](experiments/2026-10-24-one-video-track-to-many-viewers-on-linux.md#xmoq-watch-on-linux)), with decoders that are decided (`openh264`, `opus-rs` since 2026-10-24) and a sound output that is not ([its README](../crates/watch/README.md)) |
| Ghostty video, audio, chat | Validation required: spikes wrote a broadcast as Kitty graphics and played its sound, with no terminal ([record](experiments/2026-10-11-a-broadcast-in-a-terminal-picture-and-sound.md)); a later spike plays both by one clock, joins the room and shows and sends its chat ([record](experiments/2026-10-12-the-terminal-viewer-joins-a-room.md)), and a person saw its picture in Ghostty on 2026-10-12. That spike is the crate `xmoq-watch` now; nobody is recorded to have heard its sound, and on Linux it played to a pseudo-terminal with no sound output, which nobody saw |
| Chat | Decided on 2026-10-24: **the chat goes over MoQ tracks, through the relay**, and over nothing else: the room's WebSocket carries no chat. Each participant says its lines on a track of its own; a gatherer beside the relay numbers the lines of a room in the order they reach it, which is the one order every viewer sees, publishes them again as one track of the room, and keeps them for good in SQLite, from which a late joiner is answered by FETCH. A line is at most 512 bytes of text, a person says at most 5 a second, and a FETCH gets at most 1,000 lines; these are settings of the gatherer with those defaults. A line carries both the sender's time and the gatherer's, and only the gatherer's order counts. The gatherer, `xmoq-chat`, is implemented and ran on one machine through the own relay and through `moqtail`'s ([record](experiments/2026-10-24-chat-gatherer.md)). **The demo's pages are participants over the tracks** (`@xmoq/chat`), implemented and run in Chromium through both relays ([record](experiments/2026-10-24-demo-chat-over-tracks.md)), and over the tracks alone: the room no longer carries a line, implemented ([record](experiments/2026-10-24-the-chat-off-the-rooms-websocket.md)). With `--auth` (decided on 2026-10-24) the relay takes a token whose namespace ends with the field `*` for that namespace and those under it, field by field; the gatherer is given the relay's key and makes its own tokens, in the demo alone; a page or the terminal viewer asks the room for a token to say lines on its own namespace and one to read the room's, and puts each on its request. Implemented and run through the own relay with the pages ([record](experiments/2026-10-24-the-demo-chat-with-tokens.md)); the terminal viewer's two tokens are not run. Who may get a token is undecided, as for the broadcast. The terminal viewer `xmoq-watch` is a participant over the tracks alone as well, implemented and run through both relays (#279). Nobody has looked at the chat in a browser or a terminal |
| Durable Objects for room state | Validation required: a room that says where a broadcast is, a count and reactions ran in Miniflare on one machine, with the demo's pages; the chat is not the room's (row "Chat"); the publishing page registers with the room (decided 2026-10-11). Not on Cloudflare, no authentication |
| SNS event integration | Future goal |
| An agent takes part in a broadcast | Decided on 2026-10-25: **a CLI (`xmoq-agent`, `crates/agent`) subscribes to a broadcast and writes what it carries to stdout or to files, where an agent such as Claude reads it.** Implemented and run on one machine through the own relay, against the demo as well (`mise run agent-demo`): the chat, who joins and leaves, the broadcast's beginning and end and its transcript as lines of JSON, a PNG of the video for `/frame`, and lines of its stdin said in the chat ([reader](experiments/2026-10-25-a-reader-of-the-room-for-an-agent.md), [demo](experiments/2026-10-25-an-agent-reads-the-demo-with-xmoq-agent.md)); what it writes is in `crates/agent/README.md`. The sound is transcribed with whisper.cpp by a transcriber beside the relay (`xmoq-transcriber`), which publishes the words as the track `speech` of `xmoq/transcript/<prefix>`; the agent subscribes to it, as any viewer can (decided on 2026-10-25; the transcriber is implemented and was run through the own relay, [record](experiments/2026-10-25-the-transcriber-reads-a-voice.md)). The agent says what it has to say as lines of the room's chat (row "Chat"), text alone to begin with. This comes before meetings. Validation required: the processor and memory the transcriber takes were not measured (how many voices it keeps up with was, [record](experiments/2026-10-25-how-many-voices-one-transcriber-reads.md)). Undecided: whether the form the CLI writes is the one to keep, whether the agent is given pictures, and how the people present learn that an agent takes part and transcribes them |
| Meetings (many to many) | Decided on 2026-10-25: **meetings are in scope, of 49 participants at most**, after an agent can follow a broadcast; an agent takes part in them as in a broadcast. Decided on 2026-10-25 for the first version ([design draft](scale-and-meetings.md)): **participants find each other with SUBSCRIBE_NAMESPACE** under `xmoq/meet/<room>`; **the room holds the limit**, counting the participants and giving no token past 49, and a page keeps 48 others; **each one receives the small video of 8 as they came and the sound of all**. Implemented and run on one machine with three participants: the page's side (`@xmoq/meeting`, [record](experiments/2026-10-25-a-meeting-of-pages.md)), the room's seats and tokens ([record](experiments/2026-10-25-the-room-lets-49-into-a-meeting.md)), the demo's page to meet in, `/meet` ([record](experiments/2026-10-25-the-demo-has-a-meeting-page.md)), and an agent that takes a seat and reads who speaks ([record](experiments/2026-10-25-an-agent-takes-a-seat-in-a-rooms-meeting.md), [and](experiments/2026-10-25-agent-demo-with-a-meeting.md)). Validation required: a meeting of 49, the relay's load, the delay from mouth to ear, and a person hearing it. The rest of the draft is proposed and undecided |
| Reactions | Proposed; delivery undecided |
| Video codec | Decided for Phase 1: H.264 in Annex B format. Profile, level and key frame interval are undecided ([open decisions](open-questions.md)) |
| Latency, capacity | Goals to aim at, not required (decided on 2026-10-25; they replace the goal of 100 viewers a room and 10 rooms of 2026-10-24): **a delay of 500 ms at most** (decided on 2026-10-24), **one relay serves 500 viewers of a broadcast, and relays are added to build a broadcast of 10,000.** The delay is the one the records measure: from a frame being made at the publisher to its being shown at a viewer (where a run has no screen, as with the Rust client's viewers, to its arriving there), at the 99th percentile of the viewers' frames. Against the earlier goal, one relay took 500 viewers in all within 500 ms on one machine, not 10 rooms of 100 ([record](experiments/2026-10-24-ten-rooms-of-a-hundred-viewers.md)). The first step was measured: with a video track and a track of the shape of the sound, one relay on Linux in a VM gave everything to 500 viewers, each of them two sessions of the Rust client for 10 seconds, unlike a page that has both tracks on one ([record](experiments/2026-10-24-one-video-track-to-many-viewers-on-linux.md)); validation required with real viewers and links. For the second step the own relay takes another as its upstream ([record](experiments/2026-10-25-a-relay-below-another-relay.md)); a full relay sends the next session to another (decided on 2026-10-25: 1,000 sessions a relay, provisional, `--when-full`), implemented and run on one machine ([record](experiments/2026-10-25-a-full-relay-sends-the-next-session-away.md)); what waits to be written is bounded by each subscription, by its session and by the relay as a whole (decided on 2026-10-25; 2 GiB for the relay, provisional, each bound a flag), implemented and run in the relay's tests and the interop run (row on what may wait to be written in the [implementation notes](moqt-implementation-notes.md)); handing viewers out over relays and the rest of the [design draft](scale-and-meetings.md) are not built, and measured once on one machine, over loopback: through an origin and two edges of 250 viewers each, every viewer of a 1280x720 track got every frame, the origin sent each Group twice (once to each edge) and took 1% of a core whatever the viewers, and the frames came within 74 ms at the 99th percentile, against 229 ms with the 500 at the origin alone ([record](experiments/2026-10-25-one-video-track-through-two-edges.md)); no run of 10,000, or on more than one machine. Meetings: 49 participants at most (decided on 2026-10-25, row on meetings). Measured on one machine ([summary](av-demo.md)): [delay](experiments/2026-10-11-where-the-delay-of-the-demo-comes-from.md), [delay and loss](experiments/2026-10-12-video-over-a-link-that-delays-and-loses.md), [viewers](experiments/2026-10-11-one-video-track-to-many-viewers.md) |
| Authentication, privacy, recording | A token of the room that the relay checks is decided and implemented ([record](experiments/2026-10-11-authorization-tokens-of-a-room-checked-by-the-relay.md)); who may get one is undecided, as are privacy and recording |
