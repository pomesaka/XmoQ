# The audio and video demo: what it does, and where it ends

What the demo (`mise run demo`, `demo/`) was seen to do by 2026-10-12, and what it was not. Every number is from a run that is recorded under `docs/experiments/`; the record has how it was got. **All of it is one machine**: publisher, relay and viewers on loopback, and for a link a proxy that delays, drops and limits (`spikes/lossy-link`). Nothing here was run over a real network, and a person looked and listened only twice, at the whole of it and not at single numbers (the last section).

## What it is

A page encodes a picture (a clock it paints, or a camera) as H.264, 640x360 at 30 frames a second with a key frame a second (1280x720 and 1920x1080 with `/publish?size=720` and `1080`, which ran on one machine: [record](experiments/2026-10-12-the-demo-at-720-and-1080-lines.md); everything measured below is 640x360), and a tone (or a microphone) as Opus, 48,000 Hz mono, and publishes both through the own relay with an MSF catalog. Other pages find the tracks in the catalog and play picture and sound by one clock. A room (a Durable Object in Miniflare) says where the broadcast is and carries a chat. The parts are `@xmoq/moqt`, `@xmoq/loc`, `@xmoq/msf` and `@xmoq/media`; the demo's pages draw, count and show.

## What was measured

**The sound was ten pieces a Group and a stream in every run below, unless the row says otherwise.** Since 2026-10-13 it is a piece a Group (decided; `DEFAULT_AUDIO_CHUNKS_PER_GROUP`), and the demo was not measured again with that: where 1 and 10 were run side by side ([record](experiments/2026-10-12-one-five-or-ten-pieces-of-sound-a-group.md)) they could not be told apart without loss and at 1% loss, 1 lost fewer pieces on a narrow link and at 5% loss with 50 ms, and the relay had about a half more to do. Measured again on 2026-10-14 beside ten ([record](experiments/2026-10-14-a-piece-of-sound-a-stream-measured-again.md)): alike on a clean link and with 150 ms; at 1% loss with 50 ms as many pieces too late and more breaks (54 to 62 in 20 s against 30 to 46), which three later runs of one a stream (24 to 39) put within the scatter; the relay's CPU 6.3 to 7.7% of a core against 4.0 to 5.0% with one viewer.

## What later records took back

- **"The sound falls behind on a link with delay and loss", and "ten pieces on one stream prevent it"** ([lossy link](experiments/2026-10-11-demo-over-a-lossy-link.md)): withdrawn. The sound was not late. Chromium's decoder gave the pieces times of its own count, and each piece dropped as late moved all later ones 20 ms; the player now gives each piece the time of its chunk and puts late pieces in order ([cause](experiments/2026-10-11-sound-falls-behind-on-a-link-with-delay-and-loss.md)).
- **"BBR keeps the picture live"** holds for loss, and not for a link with too little room, where it is worse than Cubic (the row above). The relay's default is Cubic and stays so (decided on 2026-10-12, [open decisions](open-questions.md)); `--congestion bbr` chooses the other. A path that is long and loses much is where the difference was largest (the row on loss with delay).
- **Every number of the sound's lead before 2026-10-12 is 11 ms off**: the demo's tone carried times 11 ms ahead in Chromium (about 100 ms in Firefox), which was the demo's fault and is mended ([tone](experiments/2026-10-11-demo-in-firefox-and-webkit.md)). With it, a buffer of 150 ms held a frame for 133 ms; now for 148. The delays in the table are from before.
- **"The sound is whole on a link with less room than the video"**, as this document had it and the records of 2026-10-12 on the delivery timeout read: true of the buffer of 150 ms those runs set (`?buffer=150`), and not of 50 ms, the default then (150 ms since 2026-10-21), with which three of four pieces are left out as too late. The records stand as they are; which buffer a run had is in the "Environment" or the command of each ([sound and priorities](experiments/2026-10-13-the-sound-behind-the-video-on-a-narrow-link-and-priorities.md)).
- **Tried on 2026-10-12 and not taken**, each with its record:
  - A timeout on the video that follows how late the sound comes: the same frames as the fixed 150 ms everywhere, the picture 15 to 55 ms nearer on a narrow link, and a pause at every change ([record](experiments/2026-10-12-a-timeout-and-a-buffer-that-follow-the-sound.md); it was run before a timeout could be changed without subscribing anew, and not again since).
  - An automatic buffer that follows the sound alone: on a narrow link it stays at 110 ms where the one that follows picture and sound goes to its limit of 400 ms, and picture and sound are then 230 to 270 ms apart (the same record).
  - Another timeout for each version of the video: nothing gained; 50 ms on the large version once kept the page from going down at all, none made the way back up fail ([record](experiments/2026-10-12-a-delivery-timeout-for-each-version-of-the-video.md)).
  - Going down by the streams the relay gave up: sooner (1.3 to 2.3 s), and wrong on a link with little room to spare, where it went down with the picture in time ([record](experiments/2026-10-12-a-judge-that-counts-what-the-relay-gave-up-and-tries-before-it-goes-up.md)).
- **A later Group of video that waits for the one before it** was tried and not taken: no more frames at 1% loss, fewer at 5% ([record](experiments/2026-10-12-a-later-group-of-video-waits-for-the-one-before-it.md)).

## Where it ends

- **One machine.** No run over a real network, between two machines, or through a NAT; the hosting is undecided.
- **A person watched twice.** On 2026-10-11 they saw one viewer play a publisher's picture smoothly and heard the sound, with crackles, before the seams were mended. On 2026-10-12 they watched the demo again, found the sound smooth, and saw the terminal viewer's picture in Ghostty. The sync, the flags and the bad links they did not judge; every other statement about picture and sound is a count read from the page.
- **Every value is provisional but the buffer (150 ms) and the watching page's delivery timeout on its video (150 ms), both decided on 2026-10-21**: the sound's least wait (30 ms), what the automatic buffer and the change of versions go by, the pause before connecting again (2 s). `packages/media/README.md` has each with where it is from.
- **A frame that does not come is not concealed.** The end of a Group that comes after the next has begun is dropped; a piece of sound that is missing leaves a gap.
- **What thins the video for a slow viewer is one thing, since 2026-10-12 (decided for the watching page on 2026-10-21)**: the watching page asks the relay to give a Group of video up 150 ms after its end (a SUBGROUP_DELIVERY_TIMEOUT on its SUBSCRIBE), and the relay does, for that viewer and that track. The frames of the Group's end are then missing and the picture stands still until the next key frame. The page can ask for another time while it plays (a REQUEST_UPDATE; the relay takes one that changes delivery timeouts and refuses every other). The relay's own flags and the two versions of the video are trials behind flags; a library user of `playVideoTrack` asks for nothing unless told.
- **The camera and the microphone ran only as Chromium's fake devices.**
- **A publisher whose video and sound are timed by two clocks is handled only behind a flag, and was not measured with real devices.** The viewer has one fastest way for both tracks, so the track with the slower clock is later and later: the sound stops, or the frames are drawn as they come and, 10 seconds off, not at all. Seen with a flag that moves the painted clock's times against the tone's ([record](experiments/2026-10-13-a-publisher-and-a-viewer-whose-clocks-run-at-different-speeds.md), which has the steps for a camera and a microphone). The fastest way of the last minute does not mend it. `/publish?stamp=least` has both encoders follow the wall clock by the least of the last minute: a painted clock or a tone 1,000 ppm fast or slow then stays where it began, or 60 ms above it when it is the slow one (what its clock loses in a minute), and the viewer plays about one piece of sound a second at another speed, with no break ([record](experiments/2026-10-13-an-hour-of-the-demo.md)). With it, the demo's own two clocks (`canvasClock=performance`) kept the video's number at 2 to 3 ms for 43 minutes, where it was past 40 ms without. Not the default until a camera and a microphone are measured.
- **An hour of the demo, with nothing in between**: the relay, the terminal viewer, the encoder's queue and the pages' timers stay where they began, and the sound is whole with every buffer (180,000 pieces: 2 too late with 50 ms, in the first 10 s). The video is 1 ms later every minute, 60 ms after the hour, because the demo's painted clock was timed by `performance.now()` and its tone by `Date.now()`: the two clocks above, in the demo's own source. With 50 ms every frame was after its turn from about the 50th minute. The painted clock is timed by `Date.now()` since, and in 10 minutes its number did not move ([record](experiments/2026-10-13-an-hour-of-the-demo.md)).
- **One run is not explained**: an automatic buffer that stood at 370 ms from before its link was touched, with 859 of 1,000 pieces too late while the link was narrow. Looked for in 70 runs and not found again ([record](experiments/2026-10-13-the-run-in-which-the-buffer-stood-still.md)). Its numbers are those of a fastest way that is a third of a second too fast, which since #219 is forgotten after a minute (a test of the core); what set it so is not known.
- **No authorization worth the name**: the relay checks a token of the room, and anybody who asks the room gets one.
- Firefox's Opus encoder gives its chunks times 6.5 ms early; left as it is.
