# Architecture

![XmoQ system architecture](assets/architecture.svg)

The diagram shows the components and their boundaries; everything in it ran on one machine, and nothing is deployed. Every client speaks MoQ over WebTransport to the relay, its chat included: the violet line is the chat's gatherer (`xmoq-chat`), which every page, the terminal viewer and the agent's reader say their lines to and read them from through the relay. The room's WebSocket carries no chat. The dashed connection between the relay and Durable Objects is **undecided**.

## Responsibilities

- **Browser:** capture, encode, subscribe, decode and render. TypeScript and WebCodecs. A page is a participant of the chat with `@xmoq/chat` (layer 1, on `@xmoq/moqt`), over a connection of its own: it announces `xmoq/chat/<room>/<name>`, says its lines on `said`, and reads the room's `lines` with a Joining FETCH for the newest 16.
- **Rust Relay:** media subscription and fan-out. The MoQ layer is implemented in this repository (see [protocol layers](protocols.md)). A minimal relay exists: it registers published tracks, answers subscriptions, forwards Objects to every subscriber and ends the subscription of a subscriber that falls too far behind. It keeps the latest Group of a track, where the track's publishers allow it, and answers a FETCH that lies in it itself; any other FETCH it passes on to one publisher, and that publisher's fetch stream back to the subscriber as it is. For a Joining FETCH it works out the range from the subscription the FETCH names. The cache does not outlive the track's last publisher. Given a key (`--auth-key-hex`), the relay checks a token of the room on every request and on SETUP. It can take another relay as its parent for the tracks nobody announced at it (`--parent`), and send a session that comes when it is full to another relay (`--when-full`). Deployment remains open.

  A slow subscriber has its own subscription ended (`TOO_FAR_BEHIND`), and the application can subscribe again and resume at the next key frame. What counts as slow is measured for each subscription, for its session and for the relay as a whole (`HubConfig` in `crates/relay/src/hub.rs`), so a track that falls behind ends alone where it reaches its own limit. The publisher is not made to wait, because one viewer that does not read would stall the broadcast for everyone. Queued Objects are not dropped one by one, because a missing delta frame makes H.264 and VP8 decoders show corrupt pictures without an error (see the [WebCodecs record](experiments/2026-10-10-webcodecs-loopback.md)).

  A fetch stream goes another way than the Objects of a subscription. The relay reads the publisher only as fast as the subscriber takes the bytes, so a slow subscriber slows its own FETCH, and a subscriber that takes nothing for 10 seconds loses it. What a slow FETCH does hold is the publisher's stream: one of the 64 unidirectional streams the publisher may have open towards the relay, for as long as the subscriber takes. The relay therefore asks one publisher for at most 16 FETCHes at once, which leaves the publisher 48 streams for what it publishes, and for at most 4 of them for one subscriber's session. WHY not the queues of a subscription: a FETCH asks for what exists already, so the publisher sends as fast as its link allows, and any queue would be full at once.

  The relay is four crates, and the form of the room's token, `xmoq-room-token`, which the chat's gatherer and the transcriber use as well. `xmoq-moqt` holds encoding and session state. `xmoq-relay-core` decides which Object goes to which subscriber, which publisher a SUBSCRIBE or a FETCH is passed on to, and what a disconnect ends; it takes events and returns actions and does no I/O, so it is tested without sockets. `xmoq-moqt-io` reads and writes MoQ streams over a `Connection` trait. `xmoq-relay` accepts sessions and connects the other three.
- **Rust CLI:** media subscription, video decoding, Ghostty Kitty Graphics rendering, native audio and chat. A first version is the crate `xmoq-watch` (`crates/watch`): what it decides is a library without input or output, and each kind of input and output is one file of its binary, the sound output among them, since it is not decided. The video decoder (`openh264`, decided on 2026-10-13) is one file of the crate `xmoq-picture` (`crates/picture`), which also makes a picture smaller and writes it as PNG, for a reader that asks for one now and then (an agent). Backends and compatibility require testing.
- **Chat gatherer:** the crate `xmoq-chat` (`crates/chat`), a client of the relay beside it. Every participant announces `xmoq/chat/<room>/<name>` and says each line as a Group of its track `said`; the gatherer is told of them with one `SUBSCRIBE_NAMESPACE` for `xmoq/chat`, or one for each room it is given (`--room`, a gatherer for each room, decided on 2026-10-24, since one session at the own relay holds only as many requests as `MAX_PEER_REQUESTS` of `xmoq-moqt`), subscribes to each, numbers the lines of a room in the order they reach it, keeps them in a SQLite file, and publishes them with PUBLISH on the track `lines` of `xmoq/chat/<room>`, one line a Group whose ID is its number, while somebody is in the room. Each gatherer has a store file of its own. A viewer subscribes to `lines` through the relay; the relay passes a FETCH for older lines to the gatherer, which answers from its file. The chat's transport, its order and its keeping were decided on 2026-10-24; its limits are the gatherer's flags. As with `xmoq-watch`, what it decides is a library without input or output. It runs on one machine through the own relay and through `moqtail`'s ([record](experiments/2026-10-24-chat-gatherer.md)). Given the relay's key, it makes its own tokens, in the demo alone. The names of the chat's namespaces and tracks, the payloads of `said` and `lines` and the limits of a line are a crate of their own, `xmoq-chat-tracks` (`crates/chat-tracks`), so that the terminal viewer can speak on the tracks without the gatherer's store, whose SQLite is built from C. WHY the chat on tracks with a gatherer (decided on 2026-10-24): the aim is to carry everything over QUIC, and one order for every viewer needs one place that sets it; the gatherer is the first of the subscribers beside the relay, before a recorder or a transcriber. WHY NOT a track for every participant read by every viewer, tried on 2026-10-12 ([record](experiments/2026-10-12-chat-as-moq-tracks-beside-the-rooms-websocket.md)): the viewers did not show the lines in one order, and the history lasted only while each participant stayed. The demo's pages chat over these tracks alone ([record](experiments/2026-10-24-demo-chat-over-tracks.md), [and](experiments/2026-10-24-the-chat-off-the-rooms-websocket.md)); the room's WebSocket carries no chat (decided on 2026-10-24).
- **Transcriber:** the crate `xmoq-transcriber` (`crates/transcriber`), a client of the relay beside it, as the chat gatherer is (decided on 2026-10-25). It is told of the broadcasts announced under a prefix with `SUBSCRIBE_NAMESPACE`, finds the Opus track in each one's catalog, and subscribes to it. It cuts the sound where there is a voice and reads each piece with whisper.cpp, one model for every track (`xmoq-speech`, `crates/speech`); what reads the pieces is chosen with `--engine`, so that another reader can be put in its place (asked for on 2026-10-25). It publishes the words with PUBLISH as the track `speech` of `xmoq/transcript/<prefix>`, one piece a Group, and answers FETCH from the latest pieces it holds in memory. An agent reads the transcript by subscribing to that track, as any viewer can; the demo's watching page does, and shows it as captions (`@xmoq/transcript`). WHY a process beside the relay and not whisper.cpp in the agent's CLI (decided on 2026-10-25 by the project owner): every viewer can read the transcript, and the agent subscribes to text. The names and the payload of the track are `xmoq-transcript-tracks` (`crates/transcript-tracks`), which needs no compiler of C or C++; the transcriber needs both, and cmake, for whisper.cpp. What the transcriber and the gatherer share (the streams they write, what a FETCH gets, the wait before connecting again) is `xmoq-publisher` (`crates/publisher`). It ran through the own relay on one machine ([record](experiments/2026-10-25-the-transcriber-reads-a-voice.md)).
- **Agent's reader:** the crate `xmoq-agent` (`crates/agent`), a participant of a room as the terminal viewer is, with which it shares `xmoq-viewer` (no input or output) and `xmoq-viewer-io`. It writes what happens in the room (the chat, who comes and goes, the broadcast's beginning and end, the transcript, a picture when asked for, through `xmoq-picture`) as lines of JSON on stdout and in a file, and says each line of its stdin in the chat. With `--meeting` it reads a meeting of the room in place of the broadcast. What it writes and in what form is a first version ([open questions](open-questions.md)).
- **Durable Objects:** proposed room/application state: in `@xmoq/room` (`packages/room`), where the broadcast is, how many are connected, reactions, tokens and the seats of a meeting, over the room's WebSocket. The chat is not the room's. The publishing page registers with the room where its broadcast is (the relay's URL, the certificate hash, the namespace), for as long as its WebSocket to the room lives, and a viewer asks the room (decided on 2026-10-11). That is the **registered broadcast**; the **broadcast namespaces of the room**, those in which the room would give tokens for a broadcast, are another thing and keep another name (decided on 2026-10-26, see the [glossary](glossary.md)). WHY: nothing is added to the relay, and a broadcast that has gone does not stay registered. WHY NOT the relay telling the room: it knows what is published, but it would need an HTTP client, a secret and a mapping of namespaces to rooms, which wait for authorization and hosting. The room believes the page, and it ran in Miniflare only ([record](experiments/2026-10-11-durable-objects-room.md)). A Durable Object can neither accept nor open a WebTransport session.

## The roles of an endpoint

Decided on 2026-10-26, not implemented. Three jobs are the same in every client, whatever it carries: serving a track of its own, keeping and following the tracks of others, and going on from one session to the next. Each has one owner in each language, below the applications, and is named by the roles of draft 18 (§1.2: Publisher, Subscriber). An application says only what it writes, how it reads a payload and what range of the past it asks for.

| Job | What it decides | Rust | TypeScript | Where it is today |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Published track**: a track this endpoint is the publisher of | Taking or refusing a SUBSCRIBE, the PUBLISH and its PUBLISH_OK, a Group on a subgroup stream, answering or refusing a FETCH, the Stream Count of PUBLISH_DONE and the wait at the end; the requests of the peer passed to the track by its Full Track Name | `xmoq-publisher` | `@xmoq/tracks` (planned) | the Rust client (`published.rs`) beside `xmoq-publisher`, which it answers a FETCH by; the video and the sound publishers of `@xmoq/media`, the catalog's of `@xmoq/msf` and the answer to `said` of `@xmoq/chat` |
| **Kept track**: a track of another endpoint, kept on a session | The SUBSCRIBE and its answer, asking again after an end or a refusal, a request a GOAWAY ended, what giving up ends in the session, a malformed track (§2.4.2) | `xmoq-subscriber` (planned) | `@xmoq/tracks` (planned) | `KeptTrack` of `xmoq-viewer-io`, `SaidAgain` of the gatherer, the catalog and the sound of the transcriber; `stayOnSession` and `keepTrackOnSession` of `@xmoq/media`, the loops of `@xmoq/chat`, `@xmoq/transcript` and `@xmoq/meeting` |
| **Followed track**: a kept track of one numbered item a Group, read from a point in its past | A Joining FETCH for the past, the live Objects after it, each item once and in order by its Group ID, and the gap between them | `xmoq-subscriber` (planned) | `@xmoq/tracks` (planned) | `Follower` of `xmoq-viewer-io` with `Shown` and `history_to_fetch` of `xmoq-viewer`; the `follow` of `@xmoq/chat` and of `@xmoq/transcript` |
| **Ask again** and **next session** | When a request is asked for again on the same session; where and when the next session goes after a session ends, the New Session URI of a GOAWAY first (§10.4) | `xmoq-again` | `@xmoq/moqt` (`createAskAgain`, `createNextSession`, `keepMoving`) | the same, and with a `Pause` for each role |

The beginning and the end of a client's session (SETUP, the GOAWAY behind a GOING_AWAY refusal, closing and waiting for the peer to see it, the signals) are `xmoq-moqt-io`'s `client`. Today `UntilClosed` is in three crates and the wait for the GOAWAY in two.

WHY: the audits found the same job written in two places or more, one of them right, more than any other fault. Each job sat in the part that needed it first (the next session in `xmoq-viewer`, a kept track in `xmoq-viewer-io` and in `@xmoq/media`), and a part that needed it later could not depend on that one and wrote its own: the gatherer and the transcriber followed no New Session URI, and the transcript of `@xmoq/transcript` asked for its past otherwise than the chat. WHY NOT `xmoq-viewer-io` widened to every client: its name and its room are a viewer's, and the gatherer would depend on the room's socket. WHY NOT the jobs in `@xmoq/moqt`, beside `createAskAgain`: it would no longer be MoQ Transport alone, and the Rust side keeps the two apart (`xmoq-moqt-io` and the crates above it).

Also decided on 2026-10-26:

- One rule for the next session, with values for each role: one for the viewers and the agent, one for the gatherer and the transcriber. All four go by `NextSession` of `xmoq-again`, for where, for the try at once after a GOAWAY, and for the pause from the try before after any other end: 1 s for the viewers (`Pause::VIEWER`), 1 s doubling to 30 s for the gatherer and the transcriber (`Pause::SERVING`).
- What both implementations of a followed track wait and ask for: a subscription that comes before the track's publisher waits 30 s (RENDEZVOUS_TIMEOUT), where `@xmoq/chat` and `@xmoq/transcript` ask for 10 s; a transcript is read on from where the last subscription left it (Absolute Joining FETCH), as the chat's lines are, where `@xmoq/transcript` asks for the latest pieces anew.
- The names of a meeting's namespaces (`xmoq/meet/<room>/<id>`), in `xmoq-viewer` today, go to a crate of their own, `xmoq-meeting-tracks` (planned), as the chat's are `xmoq-chat-tracks` and the transcript's `xmoq-transcript-tracks`. WHY: the names of each kind of a room's tracks are then in a crate of the same form, one that a part which is not a viewer can depend on.

The relay does not use the roles: it is a publisher and a subscriber of every track, and decides in `xmoq-relay-core` what the roles leave to an application.

## Boundaries and constraints

Each client uses its own connection to the relay. MoQ Track is a logical data series, not a QUIC stream. WebCodecs does not provide a complete jitter buffer or A/V synchronization pipeline.

**Parts and applications** (decided on 2026-10-26): what any application over MoQ can use is a part, in `crates/` and `packages/`; what puts the parts together into an application, and the values that application chooses, is in `demo/`, and handed to the parts. The Rust programs stay in `crates/` as tools that take their settings by flags, and `demo/serve.ts` passes the demo's values to them. `demo/` is formatted, linted and type-checked as the parts are. WHY: a value chosen for the demo and kept in a part (a default, a limit) is chosen for every other application of that part too, and nobody sees it chosen; in the application, it is said where the application is put together. WHY NOT the parts without defaults at all: a part keeps the defaults and the bounds that come from what it is (a limit of what it holds, a time the protocol asks for), and only takes from the application what the application decides.

Meetings of 49 participants at most are in scope since 2026-10-25, after an agent can follow a broadcast; how they sit on the relay, the room and the chat is a [proposed design](scale-and-meetings.md). Of it, decided on 2026-10-25: every participant publishes under `xmoq/meet/<room>/<id>` and finds the others with SUBSCRIBE_NAMESPACE at the relay, the room gives no token past 49, and each receives the small video of 8 as they came and the sound of all. A page's side is `@xmoq/meeting` (layer 3, above `@xmoq/media`); the room's side is `join-meeting` of the room (`seatFor` of `packages/room/src/core/rules.ts`), which counts the participants to `MOST_IN_A_MEETING` and gives each its tokens.

See [protocol layers](protocols.md), [validation](validation.md) and [open decisions](open-questions.md).
