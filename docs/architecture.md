## Responsibilities

- **Browser:** capture, encode, subscribe, decode and render. TypeScript and WebCodecs. A page is a participant of the chat with `@xmoq/chat`, over a connection of its own.
- **Rust Relay:** media subscription and fan-out. The MoQ layer is implemented in this repository (see [protocol layers](protocols.md)). The relay registers published tracks, answers subscriptions, forwards Objects to every subscriber and ends the subscription of a subscriber that falls too far behind (why, at `send` of `HubConfig` in `crates/relay/src/hub.rs`). It keeps the latest Group of a track while the track has a publisher and answers a FETCH that lies in it itself (why, at the top of `crates/relay-core/src/cache.rs`); any other FETCH it passes on to one publisher. It checks a token of the room. Deployment remains open.

  A relay can be the edge of another, its parent: it is given the parent's URL and a namespace prefix, treats the parent as a publisher that announced that prefix, asks it for a track nobody announced at the edge, and passes the PUBLISH_NAMESPACE of its own sessions up, so that a gatherer at the parent hears participants at the edge. WHY a configured parent: draft 18 allows a SUBSCRIBE or FETCH to a publisher "without any previous MOQT messages besides SETUP" given out of band information (§6), and it is the smallest step; a viewer that comes before the broadcast is held at the parent (RENDEZVOUS_TIMEOUT). WHY NOT SUBSCRIBE_NAMESPACE to the parent now: the relay does not read NAMESPACE as a subscriber; WHY NOT SUBSCRIBE_TRACKS: every track would go to every edge, and neither relay implements it. A relay that is full sends the next session to another relay with a GOAWAY; that relay has to have the same certificate, or one the clients take otherwise, since a GOAWAY carries a URI alone and a client pins the hash it had. How a session is sent away and what an edge does when its parent goes are [the relay](moqt-state-models.md#the-relay) of the state models, and what an edge still lacks is in the [design draft](scale-and-meetings.md).

  The relay is four crates, and the form of the room's token, `xmoq-room-token`, which the chat's gatherer, the transcriber and the viewers use as well. `xmoq-moqt` holds encoding and session state. `xmoq-relay-core` decides which Object goes to which subscriber, which publisher a SUBSCRIBE or a FETCH is passed on to, and what a disconnect ends; it takes events and returns actions and does no I/O, so it is tested without sockets. `xmoq-moqt-io` reads and writes MoQ streams over a `Connection` trait. `xmoq-relay` accepts sessions and connects the other three.
- **Rust CLI:** media subscription, video decoding, Ghostty Kitty Graphics rendering, native audio and chat. A first version is the crate `xmoq-watch` (`crates/watch`): what it decides is a library without input or output, and each kind of input and output is one file of its binary, the sound output among them, since it is not decided. The video decoder is one file of the crate `xmoq-picture` (`crates/picture`), which also makes a picture smaller and writes it as PNG, for a reader that asks for one now and then (an agent). Backends and compatibility require testing.
- **Chat gatherer:** the crate `xmoq-chat` (`crates/chat`), a client of the relay beside it. Every participant announces a namespace of its own under its room's and says its lines on its track `said`; the gatherer is told of them with SUBSCRIBE_NAMESPACE, numbers the lines of a room in the order they reach it, keeps them in SQLite, and publishes them on the room's track `lines`, from which it answers a FETCH for older lines. The names and payloads of the tracks are [`xmoq-chat-tracks`](../crates/chat-tracks/README.md). WHY the chat on tracks with a gatherer: the aim is to carry everything over QUIC, and one order for every viewer needs one place that sets it; the gatherer is the first of the subscribers beside the relay, before a recorder or a transcriber. WHY NOT a track for every participant read by every viewer ([record](experiments/2026-10-12-chat-as-moq-tracks-beside-the-rooms-websocket.md)): the viewers did not show the lines in one order, and the history lasted only while each participant stayed.
- **Transcriber:** the crate `xmoq-transcriber` (`crates/transcriber`), a client of the relay beside it, as the chat gatherer is. It is told of the broadcasts announced under a prefix with SUBSCRIBE_NAMESPACE, subscribes to the Opus track of each, reads the sound with whisper.cpp (`xmoq-speech`), and publishes the words on a track of its own, which an agent and any viewer subscribe to (the demo's watching page shows it as captions). WHY a process beside the relay and not whisper.cpp in the agent's CLI: every viewer can read the transcript, and the agent subscribes to text. What the transcriber and the gatherer share as publishers is `xmoq-publisher`.
- **Agent's reader:** the crate `xmoq-agent` (`crates/agent`), a participant of a room as the terminal viewer is, with which it shares `xmoq-viewer` and `xmoq-viewer-io`. It writes what happens in the room as lines of JSON and says each line of its stdin in the chat ([its README](../crates/agent/README.md)).
- **Meetings:** every participant announces `xmoq/meet/<room>/<id>` and publishes a catalog there (the names, `xmoq-meeting-tracks` and `@xmoq/meeting/names`), and finds the others with one SUBSCRIBE_NAMESPACE for `xmoq/meet/<room>`: NAMESPACE when one comes, NAMESPACE_DONE when one goes. A page takes part with `@xmoq/meeting`, the reader for agents with `xmoq-agent --meeting`; the room holds the limit of a meeting and gives its seats. A participant receives the sound of everybody and the small video of the first who came, as many as its application chooses (the demo: 8), and one who leaves gives the place to the next; each page plays the sound it receives, nobody mixes it. WHY SUBSCRIBE_NAMESPACE: the relay serves it already and says itself who left, as it does for the chat; WHY NOT the room listing who is there: it would believe the pages, and keep listing a participant whose connection to the relay died. WHY no mixer: nothing new beside the relay and no delay added, and an agent gets each speaker on a track of their own, so the transcriber reads each person apart; WHY NOT a mixer beside the relay: each listener needs a mix without its own voice, 49 mixes, with a decode and an encode of delay. What is proposed beyond this first version (the large video of who speaks, sending only speech, who may enter) is the [design draft](scale-and-meetings.md).
- **Durable Objects:** room and application state, implemented in `@xmoq/room` (`packages/room`) and run in Miniflare only. It is asked over HTTP where a room's relay is, the namespace of its one broadcast, tokens for the relay, and the seats of a meeting (the forms, [`fixtures/room-api/`](../fixtures/room-api/README.md)). What happens in a room as it goes is carried over MoQ, and not by the room: a broadcast that begins is a publisher at the room's namespace, which a viewer's SUBSCRIBE waits for at the relay (`RENDEZVOUS_TIMEOUT`), and the chat is the gatherer's. WHY a namespace a room, which the room names before anybody publishes: the room keeps nothing a publisher that vanished could leave, and believes no page. WHY NOT the relay telling the room what is published: it would need an HTTP client, a secret and a mapping of namespaces to rooms, which wait for authorization and hosting. A Durable Object can neither accept nor open a WebTransport session.

## The roles of an endpoint

Three jobs are the same in every client, whatever it carries: serving a track of its own, keeping and following the tracks of others, and going on from one session to the next. Each has one owner in each language, below the applications, and is named by the roles of draft 18 (§1.2: Publisher, Subscriber). An application says only what it writes, how it reads a payload and what range of the past it asks for.

| Job | What it decides | Rust | TypeScript |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Published track**: a track this endpoint is the publisher of | Taking or refusing a SUBSCRIBE, the PUBLISH and its PUBLISH_OK, a Group on a subgroup stream, answering or refusing a FETCH, the Stream Count of PUBLISH_DONE and the wait at the end; the requests of the peer passed to the track by its Full Track Name | `xmoq-publisher` | `@xmoq/tracks` (`publishTrack`, `serveTracks`; the TypeScript clients send no PUBLISH) |
| **Kept track**: a track of another endpoint, kept on a session | The SUBSCRIBE and its answer, asking again after an end or a refusal, a request a GOAWAY ended, what giving up ends in the session, a malformed track (§2.4.2) | `xmoq-subscriber` (`kept`, by the rows of `fixtures/kept-track/`) | `@xmoq/tracks` (`keepTrackOnSession`, `stayOnSession`) |
| **Followed track**: a kept track of one numbered item a Group, read from a point in its past | A Joining FETCH for the past, the live Objects after it, each item once and in order by its Group ID, and the gap between them | `xmoq-subscriber` (`follow`, `followed`) | `@xmoq/tracks` (`followTrack`, `followPlan`) |
| **Ask again** and **next session** | When a request is asked for again on the same session; where and when the next session goes after a session ends, the New Session URI of a GOAWAY first (§10.4) | `xmoq-again` | `@xmoq/moqt` (`createAskAgain`, `createNextSession`, `keepMoving`) |

The beginning and the end of a client's session (SETUP, the GOAWAY behind a GOING_AWAY refusal, closing and waiting for the peer to see it, the signals) are `xmoq-moqt-io`'s `client`: a Rust client begins and ends its sessions by it.

WHY one owner for each job: a job written in two places or more was the fault the audits found most, one copy right and the other not. A job that sits in the part that needed it first is written again by a part that needs it later and cannot depend on that one. WHY NOT `xmoq-viewer-io` widened to every client: its name and its room are a viewer's, and the gatherer would depend on the room's socket. WHY NOT the jobs in `@xmoq/moqt`, beside `createAskAgain`: it would no longer be MoQ Transport alone, and the Rust side keeps the two apart (`xmoq-moqt-io` and the crates above it).

Decided with them:

- One rule for the next session, with values for each role: one for the viewers and the agent, one for the gatherer and the transcriber. All four go by `NextSession` of `xmoq-again`, for where, for the try at once after a GOAWAY, and for the pause from the try before after any other end: `Pause::VIEWER` for the viewers, which does not grow, and `Pause::SERVING` for the gatherer and the transcriber, which doubles up to its most.
- What both implementations of a followed track wait and ask for: a subscription that comes before the track's publisher waits for it (RENDEZVOUS_TIMEOUT, the row `rendezvous-ms`); a transcript is read on from where the last subscription, or the session before, left it (Absolute Joining FETCH), as the chat's lines are. The values are in [`fixtures/followed-track/`](../fixtures/followed-track/README.md).
- The names of a meeting's namespaces (`xmoq/meet/<room>/<id>`) are a crate of their own, `xmoq-meeting-tracks`, as the chat's are `xmoq-chat-tracks` and the transcript's `xmoq-transcript-tracks`; the room takes them from `@xmoq/meeting/names`. WHY: the names of each kind of a room's tracks are then in a crate of the same form, one that a part which is not a viewer can depend on.

### Where the parts sit

The crates and the packages are in layers, and each depends on those of a lower layer alone (a crate's dev-dependencies aside). The layers are `crate_layer` and `package_layer` in `scripts/ci/check-layers.sh`, which `mise run check:layers` holds them to.

The relay does not use the roles: it is a publisher and a subscriber of every track, and decides in `xmoq-relay-core` what the roles leave to an application.

## Boundaries and constraints

Each client uses its own connection to the relay. MoQ Track is a logical data series, not a QUIC stream. WebCodecs does not provide a complete jitter buffer or A/V synchronization pipeline.

**Parts and applications**: what any application over MoQ can use is a part, in `crates/` and `packages/`; what puts the parts together into an application, and the values that application chooses, is in `demo/`, and handed to the parts. The Rust programs stay in `crates/` as tools that take their settings by flags, and `demo/serve.ts` passes the demo's values to them. In `demo/`, the broadcast (`demo/broadcast/`) and the meeting (`demo/meeting/`) are two applications, each of which puts its pages together and chooses their values (`pages.ts` and the pages); `demo/lib/` has what the two share within the demo, and `demo/serve.ts` starts the relay, the rooms, the gatherer and the transcriber and says which path is of which application, so that one can be run apart from the other. `demo/` is formatted, linted and type-checked as the parts are. WHY: a value chosen for the demo and kept in a part (a default, a limit) is chosen for every other application of that part too, and nobody sees it chosen; in the application, it is said where the application is put together. WHY NOT the parts without defaults at all: a part keeps the defaults and the bounds that come from what it is (a limit of what it holds, a time the protocol asks for), and only takes from the application what the application decides.

See [protocol layers](protocols.md) and [open decisions](open-questions.md).
